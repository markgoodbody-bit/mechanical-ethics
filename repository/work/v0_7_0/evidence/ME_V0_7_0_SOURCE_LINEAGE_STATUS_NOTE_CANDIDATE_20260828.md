## 1. Why any note is proposed

The source-to-reader reconciliation found that the v0.7 Human Reader carries the central human mechanism strongly, but deliberately compresses or omits some developed layers present in the separately versioned protected Mechanical Ethics working source.

The problem is not missing theory in the reader. The problem is possible **false completeness** if a reader infers:

```text
NOT_IN_HUMAN_READER -> NOT_IN_MECHANICAL_ETHICS
```

That inference is now known to be false.

The protected source is broader in some structural areas; the Human Reader is stronger as a human front door in others. Neither artifact receives moral authority from this difference.

## 2. Hostile pressure on the status-note idea

### Attack A — protected source becomes 'the real ME'

Failure mode:

> Calling one corpus the protected source makes the Human Reader sound like an inferior summary and moves authority toward the larger/more technical artifact.

Required correction:

```text
PROTECTED_SOURCE != HIGHER_MORAL_AUTHORITY
SOURCE_BREADTH != READER_INFERIORITY
```

The note must say explicitly that the Human Reader is not a subordinate summary.

### Attack B — reader becomes an internal-project navigation page

Failure mode:

> Listing many filenames, hashes, old versions and source strata makes the public-facing reader depend on internal archaeology.

Required correction:

Keep operational identities in build/audit records. Reader prose should state the relationship, not reproduce the ledger.

### Attack C — status note creates an update treadmill

Failure mode:

> Naming every current source feature or current source version forces a reader rewrite whenever the protected source changes.

Required correction:

Keep the reader note mostly version-independent. The exact current source identity remains in source-control/reconciliation records.

### Attack D — breadth claim becomes completeness claim

Failure mode:

> Saying the source is broader can imply it contains the complete theory.

Required correction:

The source remains a protected *working* source: not canon, validation, release, final moral theory or complete settlement.

### Attack E — omitted machinery is copied into the reader to prove parity

Failure mode:

> Once omissions are named, pressure arises to add chapters on every source layer.

Required correction:

```text
DISCOVERABILITY != COMPULSORY_COGNITION
SOURCE_TO_READER_RECONCILIATION != SOURCE_TO_READER_COPY
```

The note exists precisely so the Human Reader can remain selective without becoming misleading.

## 3. Smallest surviving insertion

**Proposed placement:** Appendix C — `Reader status`, immediately after the existing paragraph stating that the artifact is a working Human Reader and not doctrine/moral authority/legal standard/certification/final account.

**Proposed text:**

> This reader is a human-facing working artifact, not an inventory of every Mechanical Ethics working layer. Mechanical Ethics also has a separately versioned protected working-source corpus used to preserve broader development and audit history. That corpus is not canon, validation, or a higher moral authority, and this reader is not its subordinate summary. Some developed source machinery — including installed constraint, stewardship, responsibility across delegation and successors, accepted-loss accounting, and future-facing foresight — is deliberately compressed or omitted here. Absence from this reader should not be read as abandonment from the wider working source, and the two version numbers are not one sequence.

Word count: approximately 91 words.

## 4. Why this wording survives the pressure pass

It does four jobs and stops:

1. prevents the Human Reader from masquerading as the complete source corpus;
2. prevents the protected source from masquerading as higher moral authority;
3. names enough examples to make the distinction concrete;
4. prevents numerical version comparison across two artifact namespaces.

It does **not**:

- import Final Eight machinery into the reader;
- claim the source is complete or public-ready;
- make the reader subordinate;
- change the reader's argument;
- add a moral rule;
- add a control/authority rule;
- change the timing condition;
- change any scene;
- alter the protected source;
- create a new version number.

Do not optimise this wording through a broad reviewer loop.
