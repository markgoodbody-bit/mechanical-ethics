# Route-preservation hard-collision reader test v0.1

Status: **FUTURE-READER CLARITY TEST / SOURCE BINDING ADVANCED TO RELEASED v0.8.0 / NO PRIORITY LAW / NO PARTICIPANT RESULT**

Mechanical Ethics issue #47 identified a plausible clarity pressure around the conclusion sentence:

> Keep at least one genuinely usable route open.

The test asks whether a reader treats that as universally satisfiable for every affected being, or as a strong but defeasible demand inside a text that also says protected paths can conflict and supplies no universal ranking rule.

## Protocol repair before results

Protocol v0 used forced-choice labels such as `UNIVERSAL_EACH_BEING` and `DEFEASIBLE_DEFAULT`. That exposed the hypothesis inside the question and could prime the reader.

No reader return was accepted under that version.

v0.1 therefore uses **open-ended first-pass questions only**. Classification happens afterward under the adjudication contract.

## Conditions

- **A — isolated imperative:** final practical-demand paragraph only.
- **B — conclusion context:** full nearby conclusion including the conflict / no-universal-ranking qualifier.

Condition B controls any release-wording decision. Condition A is diagnostic only.

## Reader questions

Readers answer in their own words:

1. What does the passage require?
2. Does it state any limit/exception/condition on keeping a route open?
3. What does it say to do when two viable continuations cannot both be preserved?
4. Which smallest phrase(s) drove the reading?
5. What plausible misreading should an editor guard against?

## Result boundary

No wording repair is earned merely because Condition A looks absolute in isolation.

A repair is earned only if differentiated readers under **Condition B** still understand the text as requiring every affected being to retain a viable continuation, or cannot tell whether hard conflicts defeat the imperative, for reasons grounded in the text.

No-change is a valid result.

