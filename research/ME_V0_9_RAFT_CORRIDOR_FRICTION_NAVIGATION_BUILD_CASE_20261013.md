## An ordinary imaginary journey

Two adults are on separate small rafts in a managed water channel. They can see only the next bend. Water carries both rafts downstream whether they paddle or not. One person has a spare rope and a repair patch; the other has the only map of a maintenance landing farther on. Neither controls the flow of water, and neither is obliged to become the other's crew.

At the first fork a **wide smooth channel** appears easier. Taking it requires very little paddling. The narrower side channel has an awkward turn, visible drag and a short landing where someone could attach the spare rope and learn how to mend a leak. A sign says that downstream of the smooth fork there is no further landing before a narrowing, but the sign is old. Neither person knows whether the narrowing is currently passable.

The three choices are not merely three degrees of ease:

- **Drift without selecting**: saves effort at this instant, but moves both rafts past the turn. If an accessible landing is required later, the opportunity to prepare there is lost. The person may still be able to reach another landing; it would be unjustified to call the lost opportunity permanent without checking.
- **Choose the smooth branch**: may indeed be the best journey if the narrowing is harmless and no repair is needed. Its appeal rests on short-term energy saving and a present map, not a demonstrated comparison of full futures.
- **Paddle into the side channel**: imposes effort and possibly discomfort now. If the landing is usable, and the other person freely agrees to collaborate, it could create a new practical ability to repair and a more robust plan for the next bend. If the landing is unsafe or inaccessible, the costly detour could be worse.

A fourth possibility matters: one raft owner could **take** the other person's rope and map by force. That might increase the taker's immediate resources, available manoeuvres and practical power. It does not thereby become a better or legitimate future. The other person's refusal, losses and right not to be treated as a tool remain part of the account.

This is a model of **selection within a world already in motion** coupled with **changing capability and relationships**. The rope, repair skill and cooperative route are not merely exits in a pre-enumerated maze. Even if the world's physical laws are fixed, a person's *usable capabilities* can be constructed through time and repeated practice.

## The counterfactual test, without a magic score

| At the first fork | Short-horizon apparent friction | At a later bend | What this does NOT prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| No selection / drift | Low paddling effort | A presently available landing can pass out of reach | That drift was irrational; costs and hazards elsewhere may dominate |
| Smooth branch | Low immediate resistance | Downstream options depend on unverified current conditions | That low friction is always bad, or that the earlier sign is current |
| Costly side landing, voluntary shared repair | Higher effort and exposure now | New repair capability and mutual assistance **might** exist later | That cooperation will succeed, that both parties gain, or that a skill arrives in time |
| Coercive appropriation | May lower the taker's burden and increase its resources | Other raft owner's independent routes and safety worsen | That maximising one actor's power or option count is ethical improvement |

**Discriminating result:** the comparison is not a scalar minimisation of 'friction'. Which burden, whose world, which time horizon, available evidence, the receiver's abilities, legitimate authority and the other raft owner's consent can reverse a ranking. Waiting is a state transition, but costly activity does not automatically create value. **NO UNIVERSAL MINIMUM-FRICTION, MAXIMUM-COHESION OR POWER-PRESERVATION PRINCIPLE follows.**

A second counterexample makes the limit sharp. Suppose the smooth branch has just been verified and the side landing is unstable, and that the other person cannot spare their rope without endangering themselves. In that world, taking the smooth branch with no extra action is both practical and likely less harmful than a self-imposed difficult detour. More work is not automatically care; 'always choose a little more struggle' is no better a rule than 'always avoid struggle'.

## Established technical owners

These source families already make the mathematics better than a new project-specific friction notation:

- **Viability / controlled invariance** — Jean-Pierre Aubin, [*A Survey of Viability Theory*](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0328044) (1990). Controlled trajectories subject to state constraints need not minimise effort at each point. The viable-set reference and state constraints must be declared; this theory does not author moral aims.
- **Constrained model-predictive control** — Mayne et al., [*Constrained model predictive control: Stability and optimality*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0005109899002149) (2000). Optimisation over a future horizon under constraints and renewed observations is an established method; my raft prose does not outperform MPC or justify untested physical control.
- **Differential games** — Weintraub, Pachter and Garcia, [*An Introduction to Pursuit-evasion Differential Games*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05013) (2020). Several controlled actors changing the same environment require strategic/coupling assumptions; don't treat another actor as deterministic drift or assume they consent.
- **Stochastic/dynamic decision owners**, including POMDPs and feedback control, already handle changing state and incomplete information under declared transition kernels. A brief story cannot substitute for their predictions, control bounds or proofs.

**No executed solver, water experiment, or real-world trajectory observation.** Bibliographic owners were checked at their source/publisher abstracts; no exhaustive citation audit or new numerical model is claimed. The more rigorous finite two-entity model is separately assigned to Claude Code and must be judged independently.

## Editorial decision: NO MANUSCRIPT CHANGE

A reader may recognise the moving-water distinction more quickly in this image than in a housing claim, but that is an **untested pedagogical hypothesis**. It might also obscure the more consequential human cases by making real moral burdens look like a recreational puzzle. PR59 already begins with ordinary situated navigation and follows with the Workshop, clocks, consent and care.

Therefore the smallest result is **one optional source-only illustration and explicit negative controls**. Do not add it to Chapter 1 or create another 'friction' chapter by momentum. First identify an actual comprehension gap at tolerable reader burden. If none appears, **retain the released reader and existing PR59 prose unchanged.**

**Conclusion:** changing time, recipient-specific scope, friction vectors, legitimate refusal and capability formation fit the existing project. The useful question is whether the next move preserves or creates a future **that the affected entity can actually use and legitimately participate in**, not whether it minimises a universal resistance coefficient.
