# A World That Can Answer Back — human reader research path

**14 October 2026 · AI-assisted alternate whole-book prose draft · NOT the released Mechanical Ethics book · NOT Mark-authored or accepted.**

**[Read the continuous manuscript from the first page](A_WORLD_THAT_CAN_ANSWER_BACK.md)**

This is a 12-chapter, approximately 9,400-word narrative intended for a person who has never encountered Mechanical Ethics or TRACE. Read the manuscript straight through before comparing individual concepts. Its main reading route is a single uninterrupted story about a room, rainfall, a street crossing, people with different powers, records and times, an institution's response, later automated help, and a created place for neighbours. The same recognizable relations return at wider scales; the narrator explicitly stops the analogy when different domains require different authorities and obligations.

## What changed in the writing direction

Mark read the prior v0.9 concise PDF and rejected its workbook/case-file experience. The human correction, preserved at [COM #798 6167668580](https://github.com/markgoodbody-bit/COM/issues/798#issuecomment-6167668580), is primary editorial guidance: an ignorant human reader needs simple principles **layered into deeper meaning** through a continuous, middle-out, honest and fractal book. This draft does **not** append the previous three proposed case files to Mark's Part VI, does not present a checklist or a methodology as the reading experience, and does not disguise AI-authored prose as Mark's.

The structural movement is intentionally plain:

- Chapters 1–3: first-person room, incomplete perception, other people, the crossing and the record that omits a lived cost.
- Chapters 4–7: lived and administrative time, handoffs, custody, and the difference between helping one person and altering the next person's environment.
- Chapters 8–9: distributed power and the faster, mixed possibilities of AI systems, without granting technical sophistication authority over lives.
- Chapters 10–11: constructive opportunities and the same relational pattern at wider scales; analogies are tested against real legal, professional and political differences.
- Chapter 12: returns to the first room/window and same street, now intelligible through more of the world. No practical checklist is demanded of the reader.

These are an editorial map **outside the prose**, not an instruction for the reader to study twelve cases.

## Main difficult editorial issue

A single recurring street-and-crossing thread gives continuity but may **narrow a broad inquiry too far toward public-service maintenance**. Mark's released v0.8 contains the human original Leah/Mia opening, Amina's appeal, a historical engineering warning, the Saturday Workshop, care without possession and a serious plurality of lives. This alternate draft replaces much of that breadth with one invented scene. The pattern is meant to travel across domains without making them identical, but the resulting narrative may become over-explained. The most important human judgement is whether the reader grows with the telling or feels the same situation is being analysed for too long. Do not resolve that with additional tests or by counting keywords.

A second unresolved issue is voice. The narrative is deliberately AI-assisted, and only Mark can decide what, if anything, should be rewritten, accepted, preserved separately or rejected. The released author's actual original prose and human witness outrank this proposal.
