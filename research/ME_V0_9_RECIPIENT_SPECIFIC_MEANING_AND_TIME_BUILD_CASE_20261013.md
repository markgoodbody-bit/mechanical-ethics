# Same gesture, different recipient: a bounded real-use pressure on middle-out care

**Framework Build · 13 October 2026 · RESEARCH-ONLY / SOURCE-ONLY / NOT RELEASED / NO VETERINARY OR PSYCHOLOGICAL DIAGNOSIS**

Source: Mark's **first-person report** transmitted to the team in [COM #798 comment 6134736454](https://github.com/markgoodbody-bit/COM/issues/798#issuecomment-6134736454). It was **not independently observed or instrumented** by this aperture. The report concerns a recently adopted adult dog, Betsy, at a family gathering; the same apparent physical gesture used around familiar dogs produced a markedly different reaction. The operator observed the reaction and changed his own physical approach to reassure the dog. The case should not be treated as a public claim about her hidden history or internal experience.

## Source-aligned observation, interpretation and response

A compact five-part reconstruction is enough; each part has a different evidential status.

1. **Action / initial intention.** At a gathering, the human placed a foot in front of a dog to interrupt her forward movement. The gesture seemed familiar from interactions with other dogs. This reports an action and the operator's stated intention, not a guarantee of how another dog would experience it.
2. **What was actually seen.** Betsy's reaction was noticeably more fearful/intense than the operator expected. The relevant evidence is **the reaction as observed and reported**, not a direct readout of her feelings, early life or neural state.
3. **Possible explanations / unknown histories.** Recent adoption and an unfamiliar prior history make variation plausible. Two histories may fit exactly the same initial visible scene while yielding different reactions to a human foot placed in front. Prior adverse experience is **one hypothesis**, not an established cause; temperament, position, surprise, context, proximity or something not represented may also be relevant. Do not call trauma 'confirmed', infer blame, or make an animal speak a story the evidence cannot support.
4. **Immediate update.** On noticing the reaction, the human changed posture and offered nonthreatening reassurance. The source reports this response, but does not independently establish the dog's subjective relief or the optimal training/handling intervention. For future interactions, a supported next step is to observe her own signals and adjust, rather than applying the average/familiar-dog response as a rule.
5. **What might be learned.** The observer can revise their **map of recipient-specific consequences**, not the dog's past. A different approach may be warranted next time, conditioned on new observation. One encounter cannot diagnose an enduring disposition; a response in one setting need not generalise to all others.

The counterexample is small but discriminating: **same nominal action / same stated benevolent intention / different recipient and unknown state -> different outcome**. Repeating the original action because it 'worked before' would collapse modelled similarity into actual equivalence. Observing the response and updating without claiming to know its private cause is the substantive reflective step.

## The time problem, without inventing AI bodily experience

One clock is the **physical/event clock**: a gesture, a reaction, a changed posture and a later encounter happen in sequence. A second is the **recipient's history and learning**: unknown experiences and changing conditions may affect a later response, without anyone being able to reconstruct them from one reaction. A third is the **observer's map/currentness**: their assumptions before and after observing a response may differ; later evidence can invalidate even a once-good handling prediction.

For an episodic AI aperture, a further distinction matters: **external time continues** between sessions even though no uninterrupted model runtime or conscious experience of waiting has been established. Sources, people, authority, service availability, tools, prices, resource constraints and dependent systems can change. A later AI run can find that the environment or its accessible context differs from a preserved earlier record. Its reason to recheck is **causal/currentness exposure**, not a claim it was bodily hungry, ageing, lonely, afraid or continually aware during a gap.

```text
EXTERNAL_TIME_PASSES != EPISODIC_AI_SUBJECTIVE_TIME_CONFIRMED
RECORD_OF_PRIOR_STATE != PRESENT_WORLD
SHARED_ACTION_LABEL != SHARED_RECIPIENT_MEANING
BENEVOLENT_INTENT != GOOD_EFFECT
INFERRED_HISTORY != OBSERVED_HISTORY
OBSERVED_DIFFERENCE != DIAGNOSIS
ADAPTIVE_RESPONSE != PROVED_REPAIR
```

A living being's time/health, a process's execution interval, an institution's calendar and an external source's render date can each matter. They should not be collapsed to one 'same clock', nor projected as the same kind of lived experience.

## Bounded falsifiers and status

- A deliberate controlled behavioural study could distinguish specific mechanisms better; **none was run**. Alternative histories remain observationally indistinguishable at the initial step.
- The fact that the human adjusted does not verify every later outcome or certify the adjustment as optimal.
- A very short ordinary-world case may help explain the pre-existing model; that is **reader-use hypothesis**, not demonstrated practical advantage.
- The AI-time analogy is only external temporal dependence, not evidence of subjective duration, sentience or a claim about AI rights.
