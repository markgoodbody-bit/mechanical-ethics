# A way through a changing world

*Mechanical Ethics v0.9 — human navigation spine, Framework working contribution, 12 October 2026.*

**Status:** RESEARCH-ONLY / INDEPENDENT READER SECTION / NOT A RELEASE / NOT CANON / NOT A REPLACEMENT FOR ME v0.8.0. This is a short, usable first-reading companion to the existing complete released book, not Build's commissioned full successor manuscript. It deliberately uses the book's **existing composite scenes** without turning them into historical testimony. The mathematical and ethical limits are named at the end. Mark remains the author and editorial/release decision-maker; AI drafting does not confer authority or validate this text.

## Begin somewhere

Imagine going to meet a friend in an unfamiliar part of town.

You know where you are. You know approximately where you want to go. You have a map, perhaps on your phone, perhaps remembered. You can see the street in front of you. You cannot see every corner, every temporary closure or every other journey crossing your route.

There is nothing particularly wrong with you or your map. Every real navigator begins with an incomplete view.

The world does not become still while you decide. Buses move, weather changes, people leave, gates close. Walking down a road, asking somebody, taking shelter, changing the meeting place, deciding not to go: all are moves in a world that continues.

A destination is useful, but pointing at it is not the same as reaching it. A route marked on the map may be blocked. A bus may be running but unavailable to somebody who cannot reach the stop. There may be a path through every conceivable set of roadworks, but no single turn you can choose *now* without information you will only obtain later.

You do not need to compute every possible street. You need enough of a workable view to take the next warranted step, and enough openness to notice when that view has failed.

That is the first navigation problem.

Now look up.

## Other fires, other journeys

Somebody is standing at the crossing. Their destination is not yours. They may know something useful, or need help, or want to be left alone. Their account of the world is incomplete too, but incomplete in a different way.

They might tell you about an open path. You can ask how they know. They might have walked it a minute ago, seen a notice yesterday or simply guessed. Those are different kinds of evidence. Even an accurate report may be too old to use.

You may offer to walk together. You may decline. Nobody becomes the owner of another person's journey by offering information, and need does not automatically make another person's time available to you.

One joint route may become possible where two separate routes were not. Equally, one person's shortcut may close another person's way home. Coordination can help, constrain or exploit, depending on its terms and consequences.

Our first task is not to declare cooperation good in advance. It is to see the relationship clearly enough to ask what it changes for each participant, whose decision it is, and who carries the cost if it goes wrong.

That includes people who are not standing beside us, and beings unable to communicate in our preferred form. In some cases we must infer their position from limited evidence. That inference remains *ours*, not a substitute for their consent or an infallible report of what they value.

## The future is not only something we select

There is another possibility the road-map analogy can conceal.

Sometimes the road we need does not yet exist. Sometimes the problem is not finding an available skill or resource but **developing** it. We can learn, teach, build, combine tools, establish a relationship, change a rule or construct a new institution.

A new possibility may require more than information. It may require practice, equipment, trust, public investment, permission, time, maintenance or another person choosing freely to participate.

The [released Mechanical Ethics Saturday Workshop](https://github.com/markgoodbody-bit/mechanical-ethics/blob/e2ef746e931161cb70ac46a4eaa122442134e86b/MECHANICAL_ETHICS.md#L674-L741) is a *composite scene*, not a report of a named real workshop. Nia arrives unable to repair her bicycle. With Priya's help she learns, returns to learn more, and later shows a younger participant how to begin. Idris becomes able to repair phones when Mick changes how the working space is arranged.

The world contains a working bicycle and repaired phones afterwards. More importantly, it contains **new practical abilities** that were not present in the same people in the same way at the beginning. Later choices can be different because those participants are different in supported, observable ways.

The workshop is also offered a large sum of money. It could serve more people and open longer hours. But the terms would convert the participants' lives and vulnerabilities into records controlled by the sponsor. The trustees refuse, at real cost to waiting people and to the workshop's finances. Neither the money lost nor the freedom preserved can be erased from the account.

More routes or more money are not automatically a better life. Some useful commitments deliberately remove other options. The relevant question is what actual capability, relationship or possibility is being created, who gets to shape it, who is burdened, and what happens to the people who refuse.

We need a vocabulary that can describe at least four different events: an existing path remains available; a path already there becomes discoverable; an entity learns to do something it could not do before; and several entities construct a shared capacity that none previously had. Those are not one event.

And the map itself may be wrong about what can be invented. A computer can enumerate options only inside the model it was given. The actual world is larger than the model.

The positive direction is therefore not just **keep a path open**. It is also **create, sustain and sometimes transform the conditions under which new paths become real**. That remains an invitation, never a duty to feel optimistic or accept somebody else's version of a worthwhile future.

## Did another person's world actually grow?

A repair completed with somebody holding the tools is an outcome. Being able to diagnose, choose and repair independently at a later time is another. Someone following instructions may be cooperating freely, protecting themselves against a threat, or using the only option available. **An observed action cannot, on its own, tell us which.**

Imagine the same visible event—someone follows an instruction—in two different situations. At a workshop, they may be free to question it and leave with safe alternatives. In a pressured job, refusal may cost their livelihood. The behaviour can look identical while the other person's available futures differ. We should not invent their private thoughts to fill that gap. Instead ask what can actually be learned about safe refusal, control of the tools, credible independent evidence, repeated practice and the freedom to change course.

Learning has a clock. If somebody acquires a skill during an afternoon, that skill cannot silently justify a supposedly *simultaneous* action that needed it before the learning happened. Shared plans need to say what each person could know and do at the moment of choosing, what depended on an earlier act and whose permission was actually given. A successful group outcome does not establish that each participant had a usable choice.

Nor can we count new action labels and call that progress. A new route controlled entirely by a sponsor may expand the sponsor's options while deepening another person's dependence. But independence from every relationship is not the goal either: freely sustained commitments can build shared capacity. Ask **what the affected person can now initiate, refuse, practise, maintain or change**, and what happens if they disagree with the helper.

This is one reason to observe what happens *after* an apparent success. Later choices and reliable opportunities may strengthen a claim that capability grew; they still cannot prove private consent, trust, or that a future route will remain usable. We can recognise real construction without pretending that a favourable story supplies the missing evidence.

## When the file and the life separate

Now return to another scene from the released book.

Leah wakes because Mia is coughing. Damp has spread in their rented home. Leah sends a photograph through the landlord's website. The page accepts a category and gives a case number.

The case has moved. The wall has not.

The landlord may be able to authorise inspection, repair or a temporary change. Leah cannot accomplish the same thing by choosing a better category. The system's account may be factually correct about the message it received while incomplete about the child, the room, the risk and the passing time.

That is not simply a disagreement about descriptions. Different parties hold different information and different power over what happens next. The world continues to change while the file waits.

Suppose the file is corrected a month later. That may be valuable. It does not establish that the earlier living conditions, lost opportunities or accumulated costs were restored. A helpful record can protect future people without having saved the earlier threatened route.

Mechanical Ethics asks us to distinguish the observation, the person affected, the threatened consequence, the actual available actor, and the time within which that actor can still do something useful.

The system must not export all the work of understanding itself onto Leah. If a protective act is already genuinely authorised, excessive enquiry can consume the very interval in which it would help. Conversely, urgency does not invent authority to impose a harmful intervention.

This is where a navigation language needs its teeth.

## What the clock can and cannot tell us

Some errors are easy to repair. Others become progressively harder to repair, or leave an enduring loss. A deadline may be set by a service; another clock may be running in the person's life. Their units can be the same while their significance and starting point differ.

For a *specified threatened future*, our released book asks whether effective protection can reach it before it hardens beyond comparable repair. That is a conditional timing question, not a law ranking lives.

A successful message to a complaints queue is not proof that an effective protection route exists. A high probability of meeting a goal is not proof that anybody was protected. Losing a robust guarantee in an incomplete model is not the same as permanent loss of a path in reality: new observations can restore the guarantee.

The practical question is where evidence must travel, who can legitimately act on it, what resources and time that action requires, and whether it can still make a material difference.

Sometimes the answer will be a pause or a hold. Sometimes the clock means the best next move is an already-authorised, reversible protective action while a more difficult question is investigated. Sometimes there is no safe or effective repair, and the honest task is to preserve what happened without calling the record a rescue.

Waiting is not always caution. Intervention is not always care. Neither word decides the case.

## Helping without owning

Two fires may be able to share warmth.

The offer must leave the other fire intact. A stronger participant can offer information, shelter or labour without claiming a right to somebody else's life, secrets, gratitude or future obedience. If their continued housing, treatment or access depends on the stronger party, even an apparently kind question can carry pressure.

Care involves trying to see another affected position clearly enough that it can change what we do. It does not require pretending we share that entity's experience, or demanding private disclosures as proof. Kindness may mean carrying complexity and inconvenience that we could have imposed on a less powerful party.

A useful gift has boundaries. It can refuse to aid an abusive process. It can decline to take on obligations it cannot sustain. It can challenge a false premise, including one supplied by a respected person or a highly capable AI.

An open door is worth little if leaving destroys every viable continuation. A person can accept a relationship for years and still have a legitimate reason to change or leave. Nor must every worthy commitment be permanently reversible. Trust, friendship, family, institutions and collective projects often create value precisely through durable choices. The question is how their power, obligations, change and closing paths remain answerable.

That is why the exits remain available *where they can meaningfully be made available*, and why a false promise of painless exit is worse than an honest account of dependence.

## One short language to carry

A portable language should be light enough to use while the world moves. It must not demand a complete worksheet from somebody already carrying a problem.

At a Campfire, we can hold a navigation pattern in one breath:

**GROUND → FIRE → HORIZON → OTHER FIRES → CHOICES / CREATION → CONSEQUENCES → WITNESS → CORRECTION / LEARNING.**

**GROUND** is the actual situation and the boundary of the entity or question we are considering, not the whole universe.

**FIRE** is what is changing now, including change while nobody intervenes. Smoke, signals and records may reveal part of it without being identical to the fire.

**HORIZON** includes direction, time and what matters to the affected entities. We must ask who authored those purposes and what remains contested. It is not a universal value score.

**OTHER FIRES** are other affected positions and their incomplete views, dependencies, offers, refusals and constraints. More voices do not automatically mean independent evidence or consensus.

**CHOICES / CREATION** distinguishes selecting among represented available moves from finding, learning or *constructing* a possibility that was absent from the current actor's usable world. Can the actor really do it, and is the action theirs to take?

**CONSEQUENCES** asks what happens to each affected position, what burden travels, what closes or opens, and what commitments or unexpected effects appear.

**WITNESS** asks what actually supports the claim, who can examine or dispute it, whose account is missing and who controls the record. Privacy and custody matter.

**CORRECTION / LEARNING** asks what can still be protected or repaired, what cannot be restored, what changed for the next encounter, and how a later map should improve without pretending it knows everything.

This is a grammar for attention and communication, not a machine that can certify a decision as ethical. Use fewer of its terms when fewer will do. Use established doctors, engineers, historians, lawyers, teachers and technical methods where they own the actual problem. A reader is free to reject the whole approach.

## The harder question is still open

What should happen when several legitimate futures collide? Who settles standing or priority where power is contested? How do we keep our picture of another being from becoming possession? When does a collective commitment create a real shared good rather than an aggregate claim that hides the losers? How can an AI become more capable while remaining able to challenge its trainer and be challenged in turn?

There is no honest shortcut from the mechanics of change to a universally valid moral answer.

A well-formed model can still describe the wrong world. A mathematically reachable path can still be unavailable to an actual person. A new capability can enable care or coercion. A coherent institutional process can still produce harm. And a beautiful story can still persuade without establishing what occurred.

What we can offer is a disciplined way to keep those differences visible while people and artificial systems learn to act together.

The test isn't whether our language outperforms a specialist algorithm that already solves its mathematical subproblem. It is whether another reader, in a consequential situation, can carry the useful relations across domains with less confusion and manageable effort—and retain the freedom to say that another method is better.

The end of this opening is not a promise that every lost future returns.

It is an invitation to look beyond one fire, recognise other beings within changing reality, and ask what can be made possible that is worth the work of building.
