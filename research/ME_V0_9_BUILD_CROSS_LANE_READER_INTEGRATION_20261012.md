## Cross-lane findings the reader language must not erase

1. **One route in each world is not one usable route now.** Lane C's early/late filing (and Lane B's walking-home task) implement the observation-history quantifier: `forall world, exists route` can hold while `not exists uniform feasible policy, forall world`. The opening already says a map-route may be unusable; a cold reader should also be able to explain **why choosing before the needed observation arrives defeats an otherwise valid route**. No new mathematics: partially observed planning and strategic/epistemic logic own this.
2. **Risk and prior are choices, not discoveries from geometry.** In Lane C, a fixed model under `P(A)=0.9` passes success threshold 0.85 but not 0.95. Lane B's repaired zero-mass handling explicitly keeps prior-zero worlds in the robust admitted set while skipping their mass in probability recursion. Thus a mathematically possible but measure-zero world can still veto a *robust* guarantee. Which worlds must remain admissible, which probability is credible, and what failure threshold is acceptable require authored, contestable bindings. An absent prior must remain UNKNOWN.
3. **A lost guarantee is not necessarily permanent hardening.** Codex exhibited `K=0,1,1` along one play: learning can restore a robust continuation. The corrected Lane B note and code distinguish first loss of guarantee from world-relative `hardening_time()` in a stationary finite task. Neither result is itself a proof of harmful loss or its repair. This matters for the opening's phrase “some routes ... close before the correction arrives”: it is an ordinary conditional warning, **not** a theorem about every K=0 episode.
4. **Goal continuation does not entail protective repair.** Lane C's `correction_until` is a fixture annotation; Lane B's `correction_window()` is a robust goal-continuation predicate, not a general remedy operator. To claim timely protection for Mia would additionally need a specified threatened loss, evidence reaching an actor with genuinely usable authority, action duration/burden, and a supported comparison of `T_protect(a)` and `T_harden(a)` on the *same* threatened future. Late file correction and restorative correspondence remain distinct. Witness independence, custody, answerability, privacy and equitable burden are preserved by the conservation ledger but **not regenerated** by these solvers.
5. **Waiting and cooperation have trajectories.** Lane C's disputed-repair case permits separately authorised interim protection even while repair assignment remains contested; blanket STOP could squander time, but need does not invent authority. Its low-reserve case yields the *same* decision as the ordinary known-protection comparator (NO DELTA); inquiry may itself consume the protection window. Lane B's households model shows information arriving before voluntary consent can dissolve a beneficial pool; a mandate can recover the calculated surplus by violating consent. Lane B's stranger works even when reliably wrong, if the actor knows to invert advice: the corrected advantage condition is `max(r,1-r)>10/17`, i.e. `r>10/17` or `r<7/17`. None of these mechanics equates surplus with care or best-cost with legitimate duty.
6. **More paths is neither a life criterion nor a total ranking.** The walking-home (time, exposure) Pareto alternatives `(3,4)` and `(4,0)` are incomparable until someone provides a permissible cost order. Codex's separation case changes when the authored goal changes; no goal produces UNKNOWN. The opening correctly avoids telling people to maximise available streets or always accept help.

## One optional *test paragraph*, not an inserted replacement

> Imagine two ways across a flooded park. One works after rain; the other works when the water is low. Someone who already knows the conditions could take the right one, but you have to choose before you can see the far bank. So there is a possible route in each situation without there being a route you can reliably choose yet. Asking may help — if an answer arrives before you commit, if it is reliable enough for the risk you accept, and if you are entitled to ask without shifting an unreasonable burden onto another person. The map alone does not choose those conditions.

This paragraph is a **reader-discrimination candidate**, not a new human opening or evidence of added clarity. It introduces the key quantifier without symbols; it may instead add time, conceptual friction or hidden authority assumptions. Reject it if it worsens the matched comparison.
