# Mechanical Ethics — nearest-neighbour map (working research note)

**27 October 2026 · DRAFT · Not part of released v1.0.0 · No novelty or efficacy verdict**

## Why make this map?

Independent AI-produced critiques identified substantial apparent overlap between [Mechanical Ethics v1.0.0](../working-editions/v1.0/README.md) and earlier scholarship/practice. The right response is to *name those neighbours and test material differences*, rather than claiming that familiar components are unique because they were assembled into a new vocabulary.

This note is a bounded literature **starting point**. It does not imply that these works were read in full or that an AI critique constitutes expert review. Source records below range from publisher/catalogue descriptions and scholarly abstracts to publicly accessible full articles. The corresponding [three-case study collection](../casebook/README.md) offers possible comparisons; it does not establish improved decisions.

## Annotated first-pass comparisons

### 1. David Collingridge — knowledge and control over time

Source: *The Social Control of Technology* (1980; [bibliographic record](https://books.google.co.uk/books/about/The_social_control_of_technology.html?id=2q_uAAAAMAAJ)). An accessible [UCLA Law Review analysis](https://www.uclalawreview.org/social-control-of-technological-risks-the-dilemma-of-knowledge-and-control-in-practice-and-ways-to-surmount-it/) explains the core timing difficulty: early change is more tractable but consequences are hard to forecast; later knowledge can arrive after commitments are embedded.

**Overlap:** ME Chapter 6's hardening and correction-window account plainly inhabits this territory. **Potential difference to test:** separating the time a report arrives from the point at which a particular person's usable remedy becomes ineffective, and identifying who has authority and bears the work of correction. This may be an *application* of Collingridge, not a new principle. The whole 1980 book was not read for this note.

### 2. Jonsen and Toulmin — casuistry and situated analogies

Source: Jonsen and Toulmin, *The Abuse of Casuistry: A History of Moral Reasoning* (1988; [UC Press contents](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/the-abuse-of-casuistry/paper)); [Arras's critical scholarly abstract](https://academic.oup.com/jmp/article-abstract/16/1/29/936750) identifies the role of case interpretation and analogy.

**Overlap:** ME builds from concrete glass, child, library and institutional examples, and its five questions act as a topic grid. Neither move is uniquely ME's. **Comparison question:** Does an ME reading expose a consequential unavailable appeal or unmatched clock that a competent case-based ethical analysis did not? Casuistry also challenges who selected the supposedly representative cases. The original book itself remains to be read closely.

### 3. John Dewey — inquiry from problematic situations

Sources: Dewey, *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry* (1938; [catalogue record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Logic_the_Theory_of_Inquiry.html?id=554tAAAAMAAJ)) and *Theory of Valuation* (1939; [catalogue record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Theory_of_Valuation.html?id=ZUOwAQAACAAJ)); a substantive [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy discussion](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/) describes inquiry from an indeterminate situation through hypotheses, examination and consequential testing.

**Overlap:** ME's changing situation, provisional comparison, inquiry and consequences have deep pragmatist precedents. **Testable possibility:** explicit, recipient-specific costs of delay, constrained routes and incomplete restoration might help some institutions apply a pragmatist inquiry. Without a comparative practitioner reading this is an unearned claim. No full Dewey text was audited here.

### 4. Peter Checkland — soft systems and legitimate multiple viewpoints

Sources: Checkland, *Systems Thinking, Systems Practice* (1981; [practice description](https://www.systemspractice.org/resources/systems-thinking-systems-practice)); [“Soft Systems Methodology” (1989), scholarly abstract](https://doi.org/10.3233/HSM-1989-8405), which describes learning toward culturally feasible change where aims and perspectives differ.

**Overlap:** ME treats the analyst's boundary and reference condition as authored and revisable, not as an objective map of whose values matter. **Possible difference:** ME's particularly explicit test of whether an affected party can actually reach a remedy *in time*, and the residual burden after administrative closure. But skilled SSM practice may already include both. Compare actual outputs, not labels. Original monographs not read in full.

### 5. Responsible innovation — anticipation, reflexivity, inclusion, responsiveness

Source: Stilgoe, Owen and Macnaghten, [“Developing a framework for responsible innovation”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.respol.2013.05.008), *Research Policy* 42(9), 2013, pp. 1568–1580. Its accessible paper develops four linked dimensions, including practical governance of an emerging technological project.

**Overlap:** ME's anticipatory route analysis, attributed viewpoints, correction and adaptation have strong existing neighbours. Responsible-innovation practice has a field history the untested ME reader does not. **Complement to test:** whether ME's witness/receipt/effective-protection distinction improves detection of ineffective challenge processes within an established responsible-innovation assessment. The article, not all underlying literature, was examined.

### 6. Reliability engineering — postmortems and accountable corrective action

Source: Google's public [SRE Workbook, “Postmortem Culture: Learning from Failure”](https://sre.google/workbook/postmortem-culture/), which describes evidence-based, blameless analysis, accountable action items and verifiable effects.

**Overlap:** ME Chapter 9's distinction between checks specified, performed and capable of detecting a particular failure belongs near mature incident-review practice. **Question:** Does ME improve visibility of harm carried by non-users, contested authority or lost future opportunities after a technically verified repair? SRE already values user impact; never assume the professional method neglects it.

### 7. Gawande — checklists as an existing portable intervention

Source: Atul Gawande, *The Checklist Manifesto* (2009/2010; [publisher overview and excerpt](https://academic.macmillan.com/academictrade/9780312430009/thechecklistmanifesto/)); [AHRQ Patient Safety Network summary](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/checklist-manifesto-how-get-things-right).

**Overlap:** The five questions can themselves function as a checklist. Calling the work a language rather than a checklist does not show it is more useful. **Test:** compare against the actual checklist or practice a qualified practitioner already uses. If ME merely adds time without revealing something decision-relevant, existing practice wins.

## What would establish a useful difference?

For **one voluntary real institutional/practitioner comparison**, observe the existing procedure and its findings *before* adding the five questions. Preserve a short baseline with consent and appropriate privacy; do not recruit vulnerable people to prove an academic point. Observe, without assuming ME succeeds:

- **EXISTING_METHOD_BETTER:** the prior method is clearer, safer or less burdensome for the same consequence.
- **NO_MATERIAL_DIFFERENCE:** the same consequential route, clock, unknown, burden and worthwhile option were already visible.
- **MATERIAL_ADDITION:** ME surfaces a previously missed, corroborable factor relevant to a real choice or effective correction.
- **INDETERMINATE:** access, evidence or legitimate comparison is insufficient.

Record who supplied the affected viewpoint and evidence, what was already known, whether any claim was invented, and which opportunities genuinely changed. Identifying a previously unknown question is not automatically demonstrating an improved outcome. One result does not generalise to every domain.

## Evidence ceiling and editorial boundary

This map is a **source-indexed scaffold**, not a finished scholarly survey or an endorsement of the project's originality. Full text comparisons, page-specific attribution and critiques from the actual traditions are still missing. The precise contribution may turn out to be a portable **educational synthesis** rather than a new ethical theory; that is an acceptable outcome.

A later v1.1 treatment should acknowledge established concepts before staking any narrower new claim and should state which stronger existing methods were available. The 14-chapter released manuscript, figures, PDF, tags, casebook text and rights remain unchanged. No new licence, field-efficacy assertion, or invitation to disclose private case histories is created by this draft.
