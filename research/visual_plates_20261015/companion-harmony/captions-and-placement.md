## Who draws the map?

**Proposed caption.** The assessor's comparison selects a view of opening hours, while a visitor's missed bus sits outside it. The proposed challenge route brings that consequence towards review, rather than a predetermined verdict.

**Short alt.** An assessor's opening-hours account and a visitor's missed bus take different routes towards review.

**Long description.** An assessor is linked to a page outline comparing opening hours with the current timetable. The page is labelled Selected account, marking a selected view rather than the boundary of the world. Below and outside it, a visitor's missed last bus is identified with a small copper account mark. One fine arrow carries the selected account towards Review; another curved line labelled Challenge approaches review from the visitor's consequence. No arrow leaves Review with an approval, rejection or remedy. The drawing is a hypothetical relation, not evidence that an actual complaint route exists or is safe and usable. A suitably authorised receiver, consideration of evidence and a real challenge path still need establishing; no legal veto or malicious motive is inferred.

**Source.** DEV2 at `4fa7412141299a2c30b4765d01bee28106db3a4b`, Chapter 8 lines 227–243 (authored scope, alternative, horizon, last bus and contesting the assessor), Chapter 9 lines 287–297 (safe recourse, answerability, authority). Corresponding RC3A Chapters 8–9 and original PR76 plate/caption remain the ancestry. Immutable full-file hashes and line sentinels are retained in `../source-map.json` and rechecked in the receipt.

**Placement advice.** Optional after the Chapter 8 discussion of the comparison's author and scope, or beside the Chapter 9 safe-challenge passage, not both. Keep the receiver/authority/safety qualifications in the prose. Do not describe the arrows as observed operation or the reviewer as automatically independent. A challenge may expose a narrower account, but does not by itself prove it wrong.

## Growing room

**Proposed caption.** Three imagined moments revisit the child, glass and pot: noticing, practising with help, and encountering another plant. Tools, relationships and upkeep can support learning while leaving the learner free to change direction.

**Short alt.** A child notices a pot, practises with an adult and a tray, then encounters another plant; continuing upkeep remains visible.

**Long description.** Three unboxed line vignettes repeat the same window, table, glass, child and original pot. First there is a planted seed and a child looking towards it. The second adds an adult offering help, a sprout and a tray beneath the pot. In the third the original plant and tray remain, another plant sits nearby and the adult is not drawn. This change of subject offers a different task; the absence of an adult in a picture is not proof of independent performance or consent. A sentence underneath says Someone keeps the room usable. The figures and small curved plant forms are original schematic line work, not documentary observations, polished narrative painting or a performance chart. Colour is unnecessary to the distinction. Learning, retention and transfer require evidence outside the drawing.

**Source.** DEV2 Chapter 1 lines 17–29 and Chapter 11 lines 355–375; RC3A Chapter 1 line 23, Chapter 4 lines 91–97, Chapter 11 lines 333–343. These supply conditional growth, practice/carriers, another plant, upkeep and the learner's agency. Source anchors are edition-specific and retained in `../source-map.json`; DEV3 placement needs its own current text check.

**Placement advice.** Optional in Chapter 11, where the child returns to the plant, not as a flowchart of guaranteed advancement. Keep the existing body treatment of actual performance/retention, choice and exit, play and companionship as present goods, maintenance and fair burdens. It is not a reason to replace a human scene with a checklist, prescribe coercive practice or claim child learning from a new leaf.

## Common art direction and limits

Originals are preserved. New figures use the working v3 language: installed Georgia regular/italic, charcoal `#27312F`, one restrained copper accent `#A35C3C`, white page, thin rounded linework, no bold slab, full-page wash, stock icons or new legend. The map's page outline represents the selected account rather than a generic process box; Growing room remains modest narrative vignettes rather than nodes and arrows. Neither is claimed to be finished hand-drawn fine art or aesthetically approved by the human editor.

Both are 126mm × 78.4mm, against the originals' 126mm × 106.4mm. Minimum label size is approximately 11.91pt, not reduced to fit. Working font availability is verified locally; final publication typography and licences are not settled, and no font files are distributed. Mark retains art/placement approval. Physical printing, actual novice comprehension and assistive-technology behaviour remain untested. No book PDF, master edit, release, hosting, external art service or spend follows from this proposal.
