## 1 — Two clocks, one start

**Caption.** For one proposed course, protection must take effect strictly before its specified threatened loss hardens. Acknowledgement is a different event. The same usable payment may precede one loss yet arrive after housing has already been lost. Both paths use one declared starting event. Where the relevant origin, effect or bounds lack support, the timing window remains UNKNOWN; that does not itself choose continuation or a hold. Necessary timing is not sufficient evidence of success, authority or ethical justification.

**Short alt.** Two paths share a start. Funds arrive before one loss hardens, but after a coupled housing loss; acknowledgement is separate.

**Long description.** Two left-to-right horizontal lines share a vertical origin labelled t0. On the first, a hollow acknowledgement circle appears before a filled funds-usable circle; a later vertical tick marks the specified payment-related loss hardening. On the second, the same funds-usable event has the same x-position but a housing-loss tick comes earlier. This is an invented ordering example with no numerical times, not evidence from a benefits case. The UNKNOWN panel states that unsupported starting events or bounds establish no window and order neither stopping nor continuation. For the actual action, reassess any hold that changes the hardening boundary, and assess other exposed people and losses separately. Equality is not the depicted prevention condition: strict before is required.

**Source.** Frozen ME09-RC3A-20261015, Chapter 6; ME10-DEV1-20261015 lines 149–169, especially 153–159 (condition/origin/UNKNOWN) and 163 (payment, housing, care). Exact immutable links and hashes are in source-map.json. Geometry, the two selected loss points and marker legend are original explanatory additions, not source measurements.

**Falsifiers / cautions.** If the two clocks use incompatible origins, or acknowledgement is mistaken for protective effect, the visual comparison fails. A payment before one loss does not establish restoration of housing or care. Any actual claim requires its own declared criterion, who chose it, affected scope, comparable times and evidence. An unknown or disputed estimate must not be silently plotted as a known point.

## 2 — Who draws the map?

**Caption.** An institution's comparison selects a scope, alternative and period. Evening opening hours can increase while a visitor still misses the last bus. The visitor's account can challenge what the selected comparison leaves out. A route to an authorised reviewer offers a place to consider evidence, not a guaranteed remedy or a legal veto for every challenger.

**Short alt.** A selected opening-hours account and a visitor's missed bus feed an authorised review through different routes.

**Long description.** An institution or assessor points down to a page labelled selected account: evening opening hours versus the current timetable, with questions about whose scope and which period. This page sends information to a reviewer. Below and outside that selected page stand a visitor and a bus labelled last bus already left. A second arrow labelled evidence/challenge route points toward the authorised reviewer. The selected page is not drawn as the border of reality. Neither arrow returns an approved outcome; review can reveal a narrower account, disagreement, supported refusal, correction or missing evidence. The route is illustrative, not a finding that a particular institution supplies safe effective recourse. No actor is drawn as dishonest.

**Source.** ME10-DEV1-20261015 Chapter 8 lines 225–241 (baseline, scope, horizon, assessor) and Chapter 9 lines 285–295 (safe challenge, answerability, authority); corresponding frozen RC3A Chapters 8–9. The simplified selected-page/visitor layout is an original depiction of these relations, not an empirical institutional diagram.

**Falsifiers / cautions.** A complaint form may exist without being safe, usable or connected to someone empowered to change the process. A challenge does not prove the assessment wrong; a controller's declaration does not independently establish it right. A map's selected omissions are not proof of malicious motive. Standing, lawful disclosure and who may decide remain domain questions.

## 3 — Growing room

**Caption.** The same child, glass, window and planted pot are revisited: noticing conditions, practising with help and feedback, then trying another plant. Tools, relationships and continuing upkeep may support a new capability while leaving room to refuse or change direction. This is one possible path, not automatic progress or evidence that learning occurred.

**Short alt.** Three schematic scenes: notice a planted pot, practise with help and a tray, then try another plant while the room remains supported.

**Long description.** Three panels repeat a window, table, water glass, child and pot. The first shows a seed with water, light and time. The second adds a sprout, a protective tray and an adult beside the child; its label names tools, help and feedback. The third includes the first plant and a different planted pot, suggesting a new task the child may choose. A shared ground rule and final line make upkeep visible: someone keeps the room usable. The labels explicitly say practice may form capability and can fail, and support leaves room to refuse, change course or outgrow it. No growth rate, performance score, forced attendance, ownership of the child's future or guaranteed mastery is represented. Evidence of retention or transfer would have to come from actual performance, not the drawing.

**Source.** ME10-DEV1-20261015 Chapter 1 lines 17–29 and Chapter 11 lines 353–373; frozen RC3A Chapter 1 line 23, Chapter 4 lines 91–97, Chapter 11 lines 333–343; Chapter 14 supplies the return-to-room placement option. The three drawn moments and icons are original schematic composition, not observed events.

**Falsifiers / cautions.** More sessions, obedient repetition or a sprouting plant do not alone demonstrate the child's independent performance. Coerced practice, disappearing tools, unsafe correction or unacknowledged maintenance burdens can defeat the constructive claim. The final pot suggests a test of transfer, not that transfer has passed. Present play and companionship matter without needing to justify themselves as future productivity.
