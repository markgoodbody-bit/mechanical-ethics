# Field B — repairing together can create a new capacity

**Mechanical Ethics v0.9 applied research · 13 October 2026 · RESEARCH ONLY.** Public published-source reading and one declared invented contrast. Not a report of visiting a Repair Café, interviewing anyone, or testing a programme. No organisation has been contacted and no partnership or permission is claimed.

## Start with the people already doing the work

Repair Café International describes free neighbourhood gatherings in which visitors bring broken objects, specialist volunteers bring tools and experience, and people repair things *together*. Its public house rules say visitors should carry out repairs themselves when possible, with volunteers helping. They explicitly permit experts to decline certain repairs and limit risk and liability. These are substantive social and physical constraints, not decoration to an ME model.

**Owner sources read 13 October 2026:**
- [Repair Café International — About](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/about/) describes the repair-together model and learning through participation.
- [House rules](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/house-rules/) covers voluntary work, visitor participation, risk and the repairer's right to refuse.
- [FAQ](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/faq/) covers event timing, ordinary visitor access, costs, volunteers and limits on repairs or spare parts.
- [Become a volunteer](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/join/become-a-volunteer/) describes specialist and welcoming/social roles.
- [Start your own](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/join/start-your-own/) points to an already existing starter manual, registration materials and repair tracking options.
- [Fourth Global Repair Café survey reporting, 3 July 2026](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/repair-cafes-attract-1-5-million-visitors-every-year/) reports 946 organisational responses from 30 countries and approximately 22.4% response rate; it describes volunteer, leadership and excess-demand pressures. The published estimates are survey-derived, *not* measured universal outcomes or proof of personal transformation.

Repair Café and local organisers own operational advice and participants' choices. Engineering safety, liability, accessibility and training also have stronger specialist owners. ME must not act as an electrical repair guide or a substitute for local house rules.

## One constructed interaction — not evidence about a visitor

Imagine three possible outcomes for an invented visitor, Jo, carrying a broken table lamp to an imaginary community event.

**Path A — object repair:** With Jo's agreement, a qualified volunteer carries out a safe repair. Jo leaves with a working lamp, but may be unable to repeat the method. This is a real practical benefit in the stipulated world; it does not become worthless merely because no learning occurred.

**Path B — supported learning:** The volunteer explains the diagnosis, works with Jo on steps Jo can safely perform, and names a boundary requiring professional expertise. Later, with suitable tools and a new low-risk object, Jo independently recognises a similar fault and chooses an appropriate next step. If independently evidenced, that later behaviour would support a claim of increased usable capability. We have no such observation here; the later event is invented for the contrast.

**Path C — refusal and maintained relationship:** The lamp has a safety fault the available volunteers cannot responsibly tackle. The repairer declines, gives honest limitations, and may point Jo to an appropriate professional path. The object remains broken; nevertheless, accurate warning and preserving safety may be better than a risky apparent success. Jo may still be welcome to learn from another safe task or simply spend time in the gathering, without having to become a repairer.

These are three **hypothetical** choices with distinct benefits. There is no universal ranking: a repaired item, a learned skill, safety and a social relationship cannot be summed into one ungrounded number. Constraints can create future opportunities while closing unsafe actions.

## Middle-out navigation and what is already owner-known

**Ground and fire:** Jo has an object, a goal and incomplete knowledge; available volunteers have their own time, competence, tools, duties and limits. The gathering is a local, temporary opportunity: if repair needs a replacement component or a specialist not present that day, a physical path may be unavailable until a later event.

**Other fires:** The volunteer, Jo, other queued visitors, hosts and organisers each have different positions. Extra coaching time may increase Jo's learning and reduce how many other items can be handled. Neither the repairer nor visitor is an unlimited public resource. A space offering coffee, welcome and social interaction may provide meaningful participation without a successful repair.

**Possibility distinctions:** (1) repair route exists somewhere; (2) route is discoverable to Jo; (3) route is actually safe and usable with tools/help at that event; (4) Jo acquires skill; (5) a group sustains shared facilities so future repairs become possible. These are *different* changes. Repair Café already describes several of them plainly; ME should not claim ownership.

**Choice, power and refusal:** The repairer may choose not to perform an unsafe repair. Jo may choose not to learn, disclose private circumstances or remain. The presence of a qualified volunteer is assistance, not an obligation to teach everything or a claim on Jo's future gratitude. An event's public rules constrain choices but may enable safer cooperation and shared trust.

**Clocks and carrying burdens:** Volunteer time, opening intervals, queues, replacement-part availability, travel cost and the possibility of future events all matter. Recording more detail is not automatically helpful. The network's own 2026 survey describes volunteer/leadership bottlenecks and item overload; an extra ME intake form would probably worsen such pressures unless a local need were independently demonstrated.

**Positive construction:** A skill learned, a relationship freely sustained, a volunteer trained, a local repair space maintained or a repair culture transmitted to children may construct future action fields. Their value is contingent on real capability, material conditions, consent and workload. A counted repair, attendance statistic or self-reported intention is **not by itself** evidence of lasting independent skill or welfare.

## Owner subtraction: what, if anything, ME adds

**Baseline without ME:** Repair Café International's FAQ and house rules already direct a visitor to find a local event, bring a suitable item, work with experts where possible, follow voluntary/safety boundaries, and understand that repair is not guaranteed. Its start-up resources already guide organisers, and the network already has repair-data mechanisms. This is a strong practical solution. An ME-branded repair service, new tracker or compulsory workshop survey is **not earned**.

**Possible incremental use:** In a later *authorised* reflective comparison across settings (repair café, classroom, mentoring, AI collaboration), an observer can distinguish "a thing was fixed" from "somebody became able to repair/choose independently" without treating either as the only valuable outcome. That is a small transferable explanatory language for **formation versus one-time delivery**, not a better way to operate the Repair Café.

**Single hypothetical question for an organiser, only if invited:** "Was the purpose of this interaction a safe repair, an opportunity to learn, a shared social encounter, or more than one — and did the choices and burden actually remain with participants?" No mandatory answer, logging, behaviour scoring or identity data. If that question duplicates what local hosts already understand, omit it.

## Falsification and honest disposition

A clear **NO_DELTA** is the expected current product judgement: the movement's own guidance already conveys repair-together learning, safe refusal, sustainability and community. ME has **not demonstrated** a better visitor experience, reduced time, increased repair rate or greater learning. The useful research is that the portable grammar can describe *the difference between repaired object and created capability* while preserving competing interests. It is not a reason to intervene.

The analysis would be worse than the owner process if it adds checklists or surveillance; treats safety refusal as failure; makes voluntary teachers feel obliged to spend more time; assumes everyone wants to learn; mistakes one repaired lamp for a durable skill; reclassifies an empirical repair count as flourishing; or treats the survey's non-random respondents as a global causal trial.

Only independently justified future work could establish actual capability effects. That would require a separate ethical and human authorisation path for any real participation. Nothing in this document grants permission to approach a local group, collect visitor details, use photographs, pay fees or publish participant stories.

**Disposition:** KEEP the contrast in v0.9's small constructive interpretation, not as an extra doctrine or claim that Repair Café needs Mechanical Ethics. Pair it with the housing correction-clock study: avoiding an unjustified closure of the future is important; creating a usable future capacity is also important. Existing specialists and living participants retain the right to define the actual work.
