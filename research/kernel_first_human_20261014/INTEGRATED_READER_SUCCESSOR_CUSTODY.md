## Coherent editorial intervention

The former large reachability/clock/control chapter 5 has been split into chapter 5 (whose route is truly usable?) and a new chapter 6 (can an authorised change achieve its effect in time, and who controls the delay?). Subsequent chapters advance one number, making fourteen.

The repaired composition, developed in narrative prose across chapters, is **situated changing world and affected entities → bounded view → constrained act/refusal → carriers and coupled effects → accessible route → clocks and asymmetric control → explicit authored value/authority → comparison → witness/contest path → legitimate execution and observed effect → residue and correction → durable capability/maintenance → cross-domain limits and renewed self-application**. It is a reasoning order, not an execution checklist or moral algorithm.

Specific new substance:
- Chapter 1 names boundary authorship, including different institutional and affected-person clocks.
- Chapters 2–4 link observed/inferred/unknown to actionable evidence, quiet/no-contact/refusal modes and where carriers can be changed.
- Chapters 5–6 unburden reachability, define the full detect–route–decide/authorise–execute–effect sequence, and condition a same-path timing inequality on trustworthy clock bounds. If bounds are unavailable, status is UNKNOWN. A brief automated benefits appeal illustrates *fast acknowledgement without timely effect*.
- Chapter 7 exposes who selected affected scope, value, standing, baseline, horizon, decision authority and contest route; transparent bindings do **not** confer legitimacy.
- Chapter 8 identifies comparison-basis authorship; no invented scalar ethics.
- Chapter 9 allows deliberate obstruction, unsafe complaint channels and competent-owner absence as observable mechanism states, distinct from unsupported motive claims; acknowledges where even heard feedback has no effective remedy.
- Chapters 10–11 treat irreversible residue, unrealised formation, practice conditions, maintainers' costs and the time to create worthwhile capability.
- Chapter 12 distinguishes causal contribution, capacity, duty, culpability and remedy; where no single actor has sufficient authority, a governance design may be required. No percentage-of-blame formula.
- Chapters 13–14 show a worked limit in medicine versus software and the full composed route back at the original library example. Repetition is selectively tightened without making a case-file workbook.
