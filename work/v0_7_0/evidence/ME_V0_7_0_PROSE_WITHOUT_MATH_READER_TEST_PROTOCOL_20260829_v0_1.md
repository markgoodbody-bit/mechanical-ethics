## Review trigger

## Question

Can a reader unfamiliar with TRACE reconstruct the book's timing claim, limits, and practical consequence without reading the equations?

This is a comprehension and burden test. It is not a test of whether Mechanical Ethics is morally correct, empirically validated, novel, or complete.

## Reader boundary

Primary readers should:

- not have participated in constructing TRACE or Mechanical Ethics;
- not receive a TRACE explanation before the test;
- not be selected for mathematical expertise;
- be free to stop without explaining why;
- not be asked to disclose personal harm or fit their own experience to the framework.

Record relevant prior exposure and reading/access needs. Do not exclude a difficult result because the reader is not academically trained. The question is whether the argument works for ordinary subjects without unusual reserves.

AI readings may be kept as secondary editorial attacks. They do not answer the human-reader question.

## Bounded reading aperture

Use the exact candidate and these anchored sections:

1. `How to Read This Book`;
2. `A Timing Condition`;
3. `Conclusion`.

For the first pass, ask the reader to skip:

- every displayed equation;
- the sentence beginning `Let T_{protect}(a)` and ending `A necessary timing condition is:`;
- in the Conclusion, the passage beginning `The notation records rather than derives` and ending `presented as discoveries made by the notation.`

Do not paraphrase the omitted material for the reader. Do not coach terminology. The remaining prose is the prose-only aperture.

After the first return is captured, the reader may inspect the omitted material. That second pass tests whether the mathematics clarifies, changes, confuses, or merely restates the reconstructed claim.

## Questions after the prose-only pass

Ask in ordinary language and preserve the answer verbatim:

1. What is the book's central timing claim?
2. Why might a correct appeal, explanation, or record still arrive too late?
3. What happens when several important paths or clocks conflict?
4. Does the framework choose the best path or rank whose life matters most?
5. What must remain visible before a high-consequence decision proceeds?
6. What can the framework identify but not itself enforce?
7. Was any part impossible to understand without the skipped notation?
8. How much effort did this require, and what—if anything—made you want to stop?

Do not award credit for repeating project phrases without explaining them.

## Misapplication probes

Only after the open questions, ask whether the reader thinks the text implies any of the following:

- every delay is wrong;
- an equation proves that the relevant times are known exactly;
- uncertainty automatically requires action;
- uncertainty automatically requires waiting;
- keeping one path open always has priority over every competing path;
- a late corrected record is equivalent to timely protection;
- the framework supplies legal authority or an enforcement mechanism.

For each `yes` or uncertain answer, ask what wording produced it. Preserve the wording as a possible counterexample; do not immediately explain it away.

## Second pass: mathematics exposed

After preserving the prose-only answers, let the reader inspect the omitted notation and ask:

1. Did it materially change your understanding?
2. Did it clarify something you had already understood?
3. Did it introduce a conclusion not available from the prose?
4. Did it make the argument look more certain or measurable than the text supports?
5. Would you keep it, mark it optional, move it to an appendix, or remove it?

The reader's preference is evidence about burden and interpretation, not an automatic editorial decision.

## Decision gates

### P1 — prose reconstruction

The reader can state that effective protection must precede practical hardening or comparable loss, without depending on symbolic recall.

### P2 — late-correction distinction

The reader distinguishes a correct later record from protection of the path that already closed.

### P3 — priority ceiling

The reader does not infer that the timing condition supplies a universal priority rule for competing paths.

### P4 — authority ceiling

The reader does not infer that the framework supplies facts, law, enforcement, or permission.

### P5 — ordinary burden

The reader can complete the bounded aperture without unusual mathematical, project-specific, emotional, or procedural reserves. Access support is not counted as failure; avoidable project burden is.

### P6 — mathematical contribution

The second pass identifies whether notation adds clarity, adds unsupported certainty, is redundant, or is necessary to recover a material claim.

## Disposition rule

- One material comprehension or overclaim counterexample is enough to hold the claim that the prose is self-sufficient and trigger a narrow wording review.
- One successful reader does not validate general readability.
- Repeated prose success plus math confusion supports marking the notation optional, relocating it, or narrowing it.
- Repeated failure before math exposure means the prose needs repair; retaining equations does not cure the human-reader failure.
- Do not change the reader candidate merely to make a preferred test outcome easier.
