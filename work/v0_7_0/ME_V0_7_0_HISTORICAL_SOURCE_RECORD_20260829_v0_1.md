## Frances Oldham Kelsey and thalidomide

Reader claims supported:

- FDA received the thalidomide application in 1960;
- Kelsey considered the safety evidence inadequate and resisted continuing company pressure;
- the application did not become effective in the United States;
- by late 1961 the association with severe birth defects had become known.

Official FDA sources:

- [Frances Oldham Kelsey: Medical reviewer famous for averting a public health tragedy](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/fda-history-exhibits/frances-oldham-kelsey-medical-reviewer-famous-averting-public-health-tragedy)

Boundary: these are FDA's institutional histories. They support the reader's narrow chronology and authority/pause illustration; they do not independently establish every account of the episode or validate the Mechanical Ethics interpretation.

## Space Shuttle Challenger

Reader claims supported:

- Challenger broke apart 73 seconds after launch on 28 January 1986, killing all seven crew members;
- the Rogers Commission called the launch decision flawed;
- final decision-makers lacked the recent O-ring history, the contractor's initial written recommendation against launch below prior low-temperature experience, and the engineers' continuing opposition after management reversed its position;
- the Commission identified communication and management-process failures.

Official NASA / Commission sources:

- [Rogers Commission Report PDF](https://sma.nasa.gov/SignificantIncidents/assets/rogers_commission_report.pdf)
- [Rogers Commission Report, Chapter V: The Contributing Cause of the Accident](https://www.nasa.gov/history/rogersrep/v1ch5.htm)
- [NASA STS-51L Challenger research collection](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/sts_51l_challenger.html)

Boundary: the Commission report is a primary official investigation, not a complete account of every institutional or technical dispute. The reader uses it only as a compressed illustration of adverse evidence, authority, communication and the cost of proceeding.
