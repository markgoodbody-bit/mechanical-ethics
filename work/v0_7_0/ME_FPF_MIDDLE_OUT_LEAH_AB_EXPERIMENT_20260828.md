## B — same FPF reading plus bounded middle-out / ME aperture

### B.1 Aperture boundary

The aperture adds no new entity kind, relation, evidence, causal claim, authority, permission, legal duty, or priority law. It asks whether the represented handling structure carries these contestable normative pressures:

1. affected beings remain consequentially present;
2. a nominal route is not treated as a usable route merely because it accepts input;
3. uncertainty, explanation, proof, waiting, and repair burdens do not default to the weaker edge when a controller or beneficiary can carry them;
4. the beneficiary of delay or closure is not the sole judge of adequacy;
5. technically reversible correction does not erase lived or temporal residue;
6. a dirty collision keeps the non-prioritised subject and accepted loss visible;
7. any actual ordering rule or legitimate authority is named externally.

### B.2 Middle-out application

**Situated middle.** Start with the exact handling decision as experienced by Leah and Mia and as controlled by the provider. Neither the portal file nor the household account is treated as the whole case.

**Outward move.** Follow the damp, reporting, authority, tenancy, evidence, relocation, future-tenant, and possible downstream-screening paths. Keep actual, modal, and unsupported causal claims separate.

**Inward move.** Test the provider's decision frame: why does a reference number count as route progress; why is a wider private photograph assigned to Leah as the next proof; who benefits when closure follows tenancy end; which value or authority rule permits a downstream complaint signal to count against a future applicant?

**Return to action.** Retain only pressures that change a constraint, burden assignment, accountability relation, or reopen condition.

### B.3 B result

B accepts A's immediate actions and adds three proposed governance constraints:

1. **Investigation-burden constraint.** Once a damp report, a child's coughing, and a related building-joint finding are present, the controller of access, inspection, and repair should carry the next reasonable investigation step. A wider bedroom photograph cannot be the sole gate where the provider can inspect or use less intrusive evidence. This is a contestable ME burden-placement posit, not a legal responsibility result.
2. **Non-beneficiary closure constraint.** Administrative closure that benefits the provider cannot be justified solely by the provider-controlled file state or tenancy end. Closure needs an independently challengeable basis or an explicit unresolved-loss disposition. The exact independent reviewer, authority, and proportional trigger remain external design questions.
3. **Persistent answerability constraint.** Leah and Mia's account, the uninspected downstairs condition, relocation, and any accepted loss must remain reachable after the tenancy ends and after physical repair. Any later adverse use of complaint data requires notice, a practical route to challenge the attributed claim, and an external lawful/legitimate basis. ME supplies the normative demand for answerability; FPF supplies the structures needed to represent it.

B does **not** infer that the damp caused the cough, that the rent rise was retaliation, that the landlord acted illegally, or that every repair decision requires an external tribunal. It does not decide compensation, tenancy rights, medical treatment, rent, or legal liability.

### Consequential omission / recovery

No new affected entity, physical path, evidence distinction, timing distinction, or residual was uniquely recovered by B. Current FPF already exposes them when competently applied.

### Changed action or rationale

The main immediate handling action did not change. B proposed sharper allocation and governance conditions around:

- who carries the next investigation burden;
- who may close an unresolved case;
- what makes downstream answerability practical.

Those are potentially decision-relevant, but the present same-analyst result does not show that competent FPF plus an explicit external value frame could not already reach them. The distinctness claim therefore remains unearned.

### External value rule visibility

B makes its contested posit visible: control/capacity should influence who carries investigation and challenge burden. Neither FPF structure nor ME vocabulary validates that posit. Housing law, safety standards, privacy/data rules, legitimate organizational authority, and proportionality remain external.

### Accepted loss / residue visibility

Both A and B preserve unresolved downstairs condition, relocation, possible health effects, and later data-use risk. B makes non-erasure a normative demand; A already has structural capacity and an explicit `D.4` residual route. No unique structural delta is earned.

### Overfire / paternalism / unsupported norm

The following B overreads were actively rejected:

- treating Leah's fear as proof of retaliation;
- treating cough as proof of damp causation;
- treating landlord benefit as proof of bad faith;
- requiring the same independent-review apparatus for every routine repair;
- requiring Leah to continue engagement or disclose more private evidence "for her own good";
- treating least-closing action as an absolute priority over safety, law, other occupants, or urgent repair;
- converting burden-following-control into legal liability or universal moral law.

The non-beneficiary-review and controller-burden constraints still carry proportionality and legitimacy questions. They are candidate norms, not earned universal rules.
