# Mechanical Ethics: case studies and technical analysis

**Working supplement · original three-case reading edition 26 October 2026, later online Markdown studies 27 October 2026 · separate from Mechanical Ethics v1.0.0.**

The **dated 26 October edition** examines institutional information disclosure, wildfire protective action and the resourcing of entry-level technical employment. On 27 October, two further **online Markdown studies** were prepared: IT recovery and the constructive maintenance of accessible library service. They use the same evidence discipline without altering the earlier studies or their downloadable dated edition.

## Method and evidence

Each study separates source observations, attributed institutional findings,
author analysis and proposed interventions. Counts retain their units and source
dependencies. A later summary of an earlier record is not treated as an
independent witness. Formal models introduced in the analysis are definitions
and conditional deductions, not fitted models or measured effects.

The legal case's source cutoff is 17 September 2026. Its court findings are
preliminary. The fire study uses official HTML, abstracts and specified sections;
full technical-report PDF review is not claimed. The employment study has no participant data, observed productivity or measured intervention outcome. The GitLab addition relies on two related operator accounts and mutable follow-up issue descriptions, not independent execution logs or user interviews. The NLS addition combines independent GAO scrutiny, actual statutes/rules, and operator reports of continuing service; these sources do not identify a causal programme-wide improvement attributable to the regulatory changes. The two new cases have their own named source cutoffs and are not new practitioner field tests.
Detailed limits and original source custody are retained in each study's notes.

## Relation to the main work

The studies examine whether the main work's distinctions clarify a particular
mechanism: access versus authority, activation versus achieved effect, control
versus exposure, and correction versus restoration. Recurrence across cases is
not evidence of a common quantitative law. Legal interpretation, fire modelling
and employment design retain their own methods and decision authority.
