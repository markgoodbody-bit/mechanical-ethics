# Mechanical Ethics: case studies and technical analysis

**Working supplement · 26 October 2026 · separate from Mechanical Ethics v1.0.0.**

This supplement examines institutional information disclosure, wildfire protective
action and the resourcing of entry-level technical employment. It applies the
main work's distinctions to specific systems, then identifies where the available
evidence supports, limits or fails to resolve the resulting analysis.

## Contents

1. [IRS–ICE information disclosure](01-institutional-trust.md). Historical
   institutional case: request conditions, matching branches, disclosure,
   independent detection, judicial restrictions and residual exposure.
2. [Camp Fire evacuation and temporary refuge](02-camp-fire.md). Historical
   physical-safety case: warning, mobilisation, route capacity, changing hazard,
   refuge and retrospective outcome classification.
3. [Supervision capacity in entry-level technical employment](03-paid-first-rung.md).
   Constructed engineering design study against documented English apprenticeship
   funding rules. It is not an observed employer case.
4. [Comparative analysis](04-reading-across-cases.md). Common structures,
   domain-specific criteria, causal identification limits and requirements for
   further investigation.

The [Marin implementation appendix](sources/02-refuge-implementation-trail.md)
examines published local mapping, education, programme funding and ownership.
It does not establish site usability or protective performance in an incident.

## Method and evidence

Each study separates source observations, attributed institutional findings,
author analysis and proposed interventions. Counts retain their units and source
dependencies. A later summary of an earlier record is not treated as an
independent witness. Formal models introduced in the analysis are definitions
and conditional deductions, not fitted models or measured effects.

The legal case's source cutoff is 17 September 2026. Its court findings are
preliminary. The fire study uses official HTML, abstracts and specified sections;
full technical-report PDF review is not claimed. The employment study has no
participant data, observed productivity or measured intervention outcome.
Detailed limits and original source custody are retained in each study's notes.

## Relation to the main work

The studies examine whether the main work's distinctions clarify a particular
mechanism: access versus authority, activation versus achieved effect, control
versus exposure, and correction versus restoration. Recurrence across cases is
not evidence of a common quantitative law. Legal interpretation, fire modelling
and employment design retain their own methods and decision authority.
