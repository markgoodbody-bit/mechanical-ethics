# Reading across five cases: routes that fail and capabilities that form

This addendum adds two later historical source studies, [GitLab's 2017 recovery](06-gitlab-recovery.md) and the [National Library Service](07-nls-reading-access.md), without rewriting the original studies or their published source snapshot.

## What changes when the casebook includes a constructive public service?

The first three cases were selected to examine disclosure, emergency protection and viable supervised employment. They are not replications: each has different facts, forms of authority and specialised methods. Case 4 adds an IT recovery path where multiple nominal backups failed and partial restoration could not recreate missing work. Case 5 adds a long-lived library service that makes accessible reading possible through joined institutions and shows specific documented changes to previously restrictive access routes.

A framework that only says 'harm is not repaired by closing a file' can illuminate a failure and still leave the useful next step unspecified. A constructive case asks a different question: **which complementary capabilities have to exist, who keeps them working, who may refuse, and what improvements are actually observed?**

## The shared mechanism is not one universal clock

The cases share a need to connect **an account** with a transition that produces the outcome an affected party needs. But the controlling conditions differ. A statutory disclosure test is not a physical evacuation threshold. A restoration point is not an end-user outcome; a library item's existence is not the same as being able to read it. An employment hour that can be financed is not necessarily competent support available at the right time.

Three failure families from the original comparison remain useful and can be read constructively:

- **Specification:** necessary downstream conditions were not named. A backup inventory without an executable restore path or a library programme without a usable application route can satisfy an incomplete description.
- **Execution:** named conditions did not come about in the relevant time and place. Example questions include whether recovery hardware was available at the needed throughput or an applicant actually reached an authorised certifier.
- **Model:** the description or projected effect misstates what is reachable. The most dangerous version promotes a proposal, authorisation, delivery count or administrative closure into proof that the intended capability now exists.

All three can coexist. Calling one an error does not determine who is to blame or who may legally act; those require the relevant evidence and domain process.

## What can actually be *built*?

The two new additions provide a useful contrast.

In GitLab, recovery restores a service through a chain of surviving snapshots, storage performance, correct identifiers, webhook data, tooling and people. The historical service came back online, and some database work remained unrecovered. The next constructive task is **a routinely exercised recovery route**, not merely the elimination of a failed test result. The operator's tracked follow-up shows proposals and some reported implementation, not an independently measured guarantee.

NLS shows a capability sustained over generations. Headquarters, network libraries, equipment, postal delivery and varied certification paths connect to the reader's ability to use literature. The 2016 audit found two distinct obstacles, law about braille devices and certification requirements. Subsequent rule and statute changes and device distribution show specific positive changes, while end-user access and population coverage remain incompletely observed.

These examples resist opposite shortcuts: **a damaging incident is not a complete story of its repair; a constructive programme's longevity does not certify every participant's access.**

## Cross-case falsifiers and the positive test

The framework should be compared with specialists' ordinary methods, not credited retrospectively for finding what those methods already found. A prospective evaluation should predeclare the *existing method* and measure actual use burden. Useful outcomes might be identifying a missing route with an available owner, avoiding a false claim that a check was capable, finding an unacknowledged maintenance cost, or preserving one person's option without inventing consent.

The following results all count as intellectually valid: **EXISTING_METHOD_BETTER**, **NO_MATERIAL_DIFFERENCE**, **MATERIAL_ADDITION** (a corroborated consequential difference) and **INDETERMINATE**. An elegant diagram with no change in a domain expert's account is not proof of practical value.

Specific counterexamples to seek:

- A recovery specialist already tracks application identifiers, external effects and restore exercises; ME adds only vocabulary. Record NO_MATERIAL_DIFFERENCE rather than renaming their work.
- A reader accesses NLS successfully, but our model incorrectly describes their preferred route as digitally unavailable or burdensome. Update the boundary, do not erase their testimony to save the theory.
- A constructive plan raises one party's capability by imposing unagreed ongoing work on another. Observe whether the 'positive' account still preserves both parties' options and claims.
- An apparently corrected rule leaves no measurable change because another access step remains blocked. That is not evidence the rule change was imaginary, nor that outcomes improved.
- A warning or corrective notice arrives after one loss but before a different loss. Label the relevant clock rather than calling the whole case either on time or too late.

None of these five existing studies measures the causal effect of introducing Mechanical Ethics. Their value is to offer **source-backed situations for such a future test**, including a maintained capability with observed institutional changes as well as late-correction cases.
