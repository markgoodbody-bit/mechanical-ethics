# 3. Making a first rung that somebody can actually stand on

**Positive capability formation · England/UK policy, checked 26 October 2026.**

This case combines a documented policy question with an explicitly **constructed**
small-employer situation. No firm, entrant or supervisor took part. No job was
offered, funding eligibility decided, system changed or outcome measured. The
purpose is to make a possible first job concrete enough for the people who would
carry it to challenge it.

## The world before our example

The UK Government's 21 October *Digital and Technologies Jobs Plan* sets an
ambition of approximately 200,000 entry-level jobs over ten years. Its existing
approach includes job design, local coordination and meaningful work with
supervision. An ambition is not a delivered job. [C1]

The June DSIT/LinkedIn snapshot reports weak entry-level hiring alongside a weak
wider market, with variation between occupations. It explicitly does not
establish AI as the cause. Its platform data do not represent every route into
work. A convincing story about disappearing junior tasks cannot replace that
causal uncertainty. [C2]

Nor is this an unoccupied policy field. The Early Careers Jobs Alliance already
addresses task analysis, organisational design, progression and employer–union
pilots. These are existing institutional routes to investigate, not discoveries made by this
casebook. [C3] The question left for our constructed firm is narrower: **can it
support one real paid beginning without making somebody else silently finance it?**

## A small firm, a bounded task

Suppose a small IT-services firm is considering a paid beginner role. It has a
qualified supervisor and a non-production test environment, but limited spare
supervision. The firm cannot authorise a newcomer to change customers' live
systems. A manager thinks AI-assisted diagnosis has reduced some routine work
that beginners once performed. That last statement is a hypothesis inside this
example, not a finding about an actual employer or the economy.

A candidate task is to reproduce a fault in a synthetic service, distinguish
observed behaviour from a suggested explanation, and produce a test that another
person can run. The beginner may use AI to propose a cause, but must be able to
say what was checked and what remains unknown. The supervisor owns approval of
any later live change. This is not automatically a suitable apprenticeship,
productive job or employment arrangement: the appropriate employer, provider
and legal processes would have to decide those matters.

The attractive future is larger than a completed ticket. The person might learn
how to ask for help, challenge a plausible diagnosis, make a useful contribution
and approach a different task next time. They might enjoy the work and develop
relationships. None of those goods follows merely from attendance or from
producing a polished AI answer.

## Choose a boundary that includes the work of helping

If the boundary contains only the beginner's output, the project looks cheap:
one workstation, one task, one completed test. Expand it just enough to include
the people and resources that make that output possible.

| Affected party | What they may gain | What the first account can hide |
|---|---|---|
| Adult entrant | Pay, skill, confidence, a credible next step | Travel, access needs, insecurity, work that teaches little |
| Supervisor | A capable colleague and better tests | Preparation, interruptions, review, recovery and displaced duties |
| Existing staff | Shared knowledge and a future colleague | Work shifted onto them or an expectation of unpaid help |
| Employer | Useful work and a stronger skills pipeline | Cash commitment, uncertain demand and continuing responsibility |
| Customers and other users | Better diagnosis and reliability | Data exposure, poor changes or reduced service attention |
| Training or placement partner | A viable route into work | Unsupported promises and duplicate assessment work |
| People not selected | A future opportunity or fair competition | Selection barriers, inaccessible recruitment and exclusion from the record |

This is an authored boundary. It does not assign identical rights or decision
powers to everyone in the table. A client can control access to its systems;
that does not make it the judge of a worker's learning. A beginner's account is
necessary evidence about their experience; it does not give them the firm's
entire staffing budget. The point is to keep the different decisions visible.

## A correction to the easy answer

The first reading was: **provide training money and a feedback form, and a first
rung becomes feasible.** The funding rules make that too easy.

For English apprenticeships, the 2026–27 rules require appropriate employer
support and supervision (66.1). They distinguish eligible, standard-linked
training/mentoring under specified conditions (107.6) from wages and generic
line-management support that cannot be charged as apprenticeship training costs
(112.1, 112.11). Training plans, reviews and complaints routes already exist
(100–103). The register condition in 107.6 includes stated exceptions. These
rules concern this funding route, not every ordinary starter job. [C4]

**The correction changes the proposal:** a training allocation is not evidence
that all the employer's supervisory work is paid for. Before offering the role,
the budget owner must identify which hours the ordinary route already covers,
which hours remain an employer cost, and which existing commitments will move.
If the honest answer is that the supervisor will absorb the difference at night,
this version of the role is not yet supported. Renaming those hours “mentoring”
does not supply capacity or settle eligibility.

Claude Code's internal AI premise challenge brought this distinction into the
project before this case was written. It also challenged the cost of adding
another assessment beside existing reviews. This case therefore proposes using
an existing work/training review where suitable, rather than requiring a new ME
form. That is an editorial correction from source and criticism, not a successful
field intervention. [C5]

## Follow the joins, not just the documents

A route to a useful first task would require the following connections to work:

1. A manager can commit actual pay and protected supervisory capacity.
2. An appropriate recruiter or partner reaches a willing adult who can use the offer.
3. The supervisor supplies a safe, comprehensible task with legitimate access.
4. The entrant can practise, ask and disagree without being punished for reasonable uncertainty.
5. The result reaches a competent reviewer, who distinguishes a lucky answer from a learned method.
6. The next task and continuing support give the capability somewhere to go.

Each join has a carrier: a funded role, an accessible offer, a usable test
environment, time with another person, a work sample and an agreed next step.
An approved plan cannot substitute for a cancelled hour of help. A completed
learning record does not show that the entrant can perform a different task.
An AI transcript is an account of a tool interaction, not an independent witness
that the fault was understood.

Some controls preserve the possibility rather than obstruct it. Keeping customer
credentials outside the exercise can allow a beginner to learn safely; removing
the access boundary to make the role look more “real” may destroy the conditions
under which it was responsible to offer it. Equally, a sandbox filled with busywork
can be safe and still fail to supply useful learning or paid productive purpose.

## Several clocks run at once

The firm has a budget decision. The entrant has a possible start date and other
commitments. The supervisor has current deadlines and limits on attention.
Practice has its own rhythm: a long gap can interrupt what the person is learning.
The organisation's future skills pipeline changes over a much longer period.

Those clocks cannot responsibly be compressed into one deadline. Moving the
start date might protect supervision but cost the entrant another opportunity.
Starting immediately might preserve pay while producing poor support. A firm
may need to decline this version now, or work through an established partner,
rather than manufacture urgency as an excuse for a weak offer.

For a specified avoidable loss, protection has to become effective before that
loss hardens. But no durations have been measured here, and missing a proposed
start date is not evidence of permanently lost employability. The case supplies
neither a numerical timing margin nor a universal instruction to pause hiring.

## Compare alternatives without hiding the bills

| Alternative | A plausible gain in this constructed setting | What must still be shown |
|---|---|---|
| Recruit an experienced person | Meet an immediate service need | Whether this is affordable and whether entry routes can be supported elsewhere |
| Fund the bounded paid beginner role | Create useful work and a learning route | Pay, actual supervisory hours, meaningful task and continuing support |
| Use an established apprenticeship or partner route | Draw on specialist training and existing review arrangements | Suitability, eligibility, employer commitments and uncovered costs |
| Delay or decline this offer | Avoid a promise the firm cannot maintain | An honest explanation and whether another usable route can be offered |

These are candidate comparisons, not recommendations to a real business.
The proper baseline is what this employer could actually have chosen, not an
imaginary world with unlimited mentors. The affected scope includes the staff
whose existing work is displaced. The horizon needs to reach beyond the first
successful demonstration.

No scalar **∆H** is calculated. We have no observed pay, hours, learning outcomes,
client risk or defensible conversion between them. A future comparison could
record those quantities separately; it should not turn an entrant's dignity or
a mentor's fatigue into invented units. A supported positive prospect is not
merely the negative of a quantified harm score.

## What would count as a witness?

In a real voluntary test, first ask the practitioner how they normally decide
whether such a role is feasible. Record that answer before introducing our
questions. If their existing approach already exposes the relevant constraints,
credit it. A clearer conversation is useful, but not automatically evidence that
ME changed the decision. [C5]

Possible evidence then has different jobs. A budget record can support a pay
commitment; a rota can support allocated time; an appropriately bounded work
sample can support what was attempted. A later unfamiliar task might test
transfer of learning. The entrant can contest whether the work was usable and
fair. These sources can all be partial.

Five documents copied from one manager's judgement are not five independent
witnesses. Nor should an unhappy account be discounted because it conflicts
with the institution's dashboard. Independence includes who could observe the
failure, who controls the record and what it costs to disagree. Necessary
evidence should not become a pretext for collecting a worker's private life.

## Correction needs an owner and a practicable effect

In this proposed arrangement the employer's authorised budget owner would own
pay and resourcing. A competent supervisor would own task boundaries and review.
A relevant provider or broker would own its actual training and escalation
responsibilities. The entrant would retain ordinary rights and a legitimate
voice in the experience, not an obligation to prove the experiment worthwhile.
Applicable employment, equality, funding and safety requirements must be resolved
through competent domain processes; this chapter does not determine them.

The funding boundary this case relies on is not the firm's to change. The 2026–27 rules record a machinery-of-government transfer of responsibility for apprenticeships from the Department for Education to the Department for Work and Pensions, and they point to Skills England for each standard's minimum off-the-job training. [C4] If firms like this one repeatedly find that the supervision a beginner needs falls outside what the rules can fund, that pattern belongs with those owners, through the consultation routes they run. It is best carried by the people who would supervise and pay for the hours, not only by bodies speaking for them. In automatic pension enrolment, the Institute for Government found that consulting trade associations was not a sufficient way to get input from those who would have to administer the system, and that regulations had to be changed mid-course. [C6]

Before recruitment, one correction could be to reduce the initial task's scope
and reassign existing duties to protect supervision. If no capacity can be
provided, withdraw the proposed design before asking somebody to rely on it.
During work, a missed review needs a real replacement or a safe change in task,
not merely a new “completed” field. An unsafe request needs a reachable person
with actual authority to stop or redirect it, not an AI reassurance.

A failed arrangement can leave unpaid costs, lost confidence or a foregone
alternative. A later good role does not automatically restore those losses.
Conversely, stopping an unsuitable trial need not end the person's future.
References, an agreed work sample, a different training route or another
employer may support a new prospect where the person wants one. Those are
possibilities to establish, not promises this casebook can make.

## The strongest counter-reading

An experienced provider could say: “We already do this; your additional language
takes time from supervision.” They might be right. The case is also biased
towards a technical practitioner willing to consider a role: it cannot speak
for an excluded entrant, an exhausted supervisor or a worker who distrusts the
review process. A safe synthetic task may not supply the commercial demand
needed to sustain employment. AI may be incidental to the firm's actual barrier.

The honest response is not a longer checklist. Find the existing method and
ask what, if anything, this account changes. If it creates no better action,
reveals no omitted burden and offers no clearer supported prospect, record
**NO_USE** for this application. If it exposes one real unresolved join, pursue
that join with its owner. Neither outcome is a verdict on every future use of
the language.

## What the released book contributes—and what it cannot supply

ME1.0 already discusses usable routes (Chapter 5), clocks and controlled delay
(6), authored comparison (8), safe witnessing and answerability (9), residue
(10), formation, shared capability and maintenance (11), and distributed
responsibility (12). Applying those distinctions together keeps the beginner's
future connected to the actual work of supporting it. They are existing book
content, not new primitives discovered here.

What remains **UNKNOWN** is whether a real employer has an appropriate vacancy,
whether the role can be funded and maintained, whether an adult wants it, whether
the task teaches transferable competence, and whether our account adds anything
to stronger professional practice. No independent human field encounter is
claimed. What has changed in the reading is narrower and concrete: we no longer
mistake training funding or another review form for the material capacity to
support a first paid step.
