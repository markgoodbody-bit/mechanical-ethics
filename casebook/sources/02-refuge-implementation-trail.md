# From refuge research to a local arrangement: a Marin County evidence trail

*Follow-on to the Camp Fire case, 26 October 2026. Public-record inquiry only; not a site assessment or live evacuation instruction. The original case and released ME1 remain unchanged.*

## What the trail actually establishes

Marin provides a real implementation example at the level of published planning, education and institutional ownership. It does **not**, on the evidence inspected here, establish that a particular refuge will protect a particular person, or that NIST caused the local arrangements to be adopted.

The strongest directly readable physical artifact is a one-page Fire Safe Marin refuge leaflet labelled **August 2024**. Its content treats refuge as a contingency and points residents towards local evacuation maps. That is an actual educational output, not an invented community scenario. It does not establish distribution, comprehension or use in an incident. [1]

Fire Safe Marin's current map directory states that partner agencies and municipalities developed and published FireClear maps with local and CAL FIRE grant funding. Its directory contains community-specific links. Separately, the Marin Wildfire Prevention Authority (MWPA) describes a funded cross-jurisdictional detection, alert and evacuation programme and a network of routes and refuge areas. The published financing mechanism is Measure C core funding for the listed programme projects, with additional local funding possible. Neither page is an audited account of spending or maintenance at an individual refuge. [2, 3]

This is more than a recommendation sitting in a research report: named organisations describe work, identify a financing route and expose actual public information. It remains less than a demonstrated end-to-end protective capability.

## The causal story had to change

The initial question was whether a community had implemented the NIST guidance. The chronology makes that wording too strong. The checked local leaflet is dated 2024, before the NIST update announced in April 2025. The NIST update must not be credited with creating an already documented local practice. [1, 4]

That does **not** prove Marin developed the practice independently of every NIST publication: earlier NIST Camp Fire studies existed. Nor does chronological precedence settle mutual learning or later revisions. No inspected local decision record explicitly traces the arrangement to the particular NIST recommendation.

The corrected question is: **what local arrangements can be evidenced, how do they relate to the specialist guidance, and which part of the research-to-protection chain remains unproven?** Causal attribution stays UNKNOWN. Similarity is a reason to compare, not a substitute for a provenance record.

## Owners and products, with the remaining gap attached

| Link in the chain | Public evidence inspected | What can be concluded | What remains unestablished |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specialist knowledge | NIST's April 2025 update explains advance refuge planning and no-notice contingencies | A published specialist planning approach exists | That any named local decision adopted it because of NIST |
| Local educational output | Fire Safe Marin's August 2024 leaflet | A dated resident-facing refuge explanation exists and is readable | Household receipt, comprehension or ability to act |
| Mapping and financing | Fire Safe Marin's community map directory names partners and funding sources | A public directory and an attributed mapping programme exist | Grant award identifiers, expenditure, current accuracy or complete geographic coverage |
| Countywide programme ownership | MWPA's detection/evacuation programme identifies Measure C core funds and partner roles | A named funding/coordination institution is visible | A refuge-by-refuge maintenance contract, inspected budget line or current condition record |
| Technical assessment product | MWPA's ingress/egress assessment page names its lead and technical partners and links reports and a viewer | An existing technical workstream and owner are identifiable | That a model score certifies any refuge or predicts an individual's survival |
| Residents' official route | Marin County's preparedness page routes readers to alerts, zones and local maps | There is a county-owned public entrance | That every reader can receive a warning or complete the onward journey |
| Protective effect | No relevant observed incident-outcome evaluation found in this bounded inquiry | No such outcome is claimed | Actual refuge tenability, evacuation success and comparative effectiveness |

The MWPA assessment page specifically names MWPA as lead and Sonoma Technology with specialist partners. It identifies communication, transport and route constraints, and explicitly limits the product to planning rather than live incident use. This supplies a concrete handover to an existing domain method, not a reason to build a parallel ME planning tool. [5]

The county's own public preparedness page connects alerts, maps and evacuation-zone information. It is evidence of an information route, not evidence that its recipients have transport or a viable physical route. [6]

## Can the public information actually be reached?

One complete leaflet and the Fire Safe Marin map index were readable through this inquiry's tools. Following one listed Inverness map link reached a QR-hosted page exposing template fields rather than a legible map. A listed Novato PDF returned a cache-miss error. A direct Southern Marin Fire evacuation page returned HTTP403, although a search index exposed an account of its refuge arrangements. That indexed account was **not promoted to a freshly verified local inspection**. [2, 7]

Two 2026 CWPP summary links could not be opened by the web tool; the older full plan exceeded its content-length limit. These are retrieval limits, not proof that a human browser cannot open the documents, that the community lacks plans, or that physical arrangements fail. No alternate transport was used to evade a refusal. The tool also could not establish completed map rendering or mobile/accessibility performance.

The 2026 CWPP landing page is itself readable. It reports a 2025 update contract, names modelling partners and describes public engagement, including a May 2026 forum. Those are attributed programme statements, not an independent evaluation of consultation quality or resulting safety. The attached summaries were not read. [8]

The practical distinction is important: **publishing a resource, listing it, retrieving it and being able to act from it are four different accomplishments**. Here, some information was retrieved and some links hit a tool boundary. An actual resident's successful use was not tested. That is a specific ceiling, not a global verdict on the service.

## What would close the next link?

The existing actors already have the relevant institutional positions: Fire Safe Marin for its educational/map publications; MWPA for its programme and assessment; county emergency management and local operational authorities for their respective public and incident roles. A site custodian or maintenance owner must be identified in the relevant local record rather than inferred from a programme logo.

For one chosen site, the next useful **documentary** evidence would be its current approved assessment, named upkeep owner, inspection/review date, available capacity and onward-movement arrangements, with evidence of allocated resources. An adopted document is still not a field performance result. Independent exercise or incident evidence could test parts of the arrangement, with explicit scenario and access limitations. Nothing in this inquiry establishes that these records are absent; they were not obtained within this bounded pass.

No new form or repository is required to ask that question. The existing planning owner can supply, qualify or refuse the particular claim. No contact was made, and neither an unsolicited request nor an operational intervention is authorised by this research note.

The useful correction to the original case is therefore constructive and limited: local refuge education, mapping and a funding/assessment structure are already publicly evidenced. ME does not deserve credit for inventing them. The unresolved join is from these products to a maintained, person-usable arrangement under specified incident conditions. An external practitioner may find the existing specialist account sufficient; **NO_ADDITIONAL_USE_FROM_ME** remains a legitimate result.
