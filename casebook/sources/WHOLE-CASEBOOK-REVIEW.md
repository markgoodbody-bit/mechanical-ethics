## Whole-case findings and changes

The reviewer found the constructed entry clearly distinguished from observation
and the B/C comparison appropriately bounded. The remaining assembly-status text
was stale; Build replaced it with links to all three actual chapters and a
comparison that includes the legal case's distinct contribution.

Build and the reviewer identified these legal-case corrections:

1. A deliberately misleading initial telling is no longer described as containing
   truth in every sentence.
2. The earlier IRS manual's individual handling is identified as administrative
   practice, not a separate statutory requirement.
3. Facial compliance of the agreement does not establish compliance of requests
   or the implementing procedure. The synthesis no longer locates the whole
   defect in software or equates performed checks with substantive compliance.
4. TIGTA's lack of independent validation of DHS data describes review scope,
   not demonstrated inability to see that data.
5. Restricting future disclosure is distinguished from proving protection;
   the November court restriction is not placed after the February admission.
6. Current-sounding litigation and remedy statements are tied to the chapter's
   source cutoff, 17 September 2026. No later docket status was established.
7. Numerical comparison is omitted for lack of an evidenced common scale and
   counterfactual, rather than because a particular symbol is absent from ME1.

Build additionally inspected [26 U.S.C. §7431(a)–(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/7431)
on 26 October. The chapter now recognises that damages depend on liability
conditions and exceptions, and that subsection (e) contains specified mandatory
notification triggers. Whether those triggers applied here remains unknown.
This corrects an overly discretionary notification proposal without deciding
the legal outcome. Build also read the official TIGTA report's opening findings;
no independent full legal-source audit or fresh docket investigation is claimed.

The closing now distinguishes the three evidential settings: preliminary legal
findings within a dated record, retrospective fire research, and a constructed
employment situation against documented policy. Shared reasoning questions do
not imply shared legal, physical or employment thresholds, nor prove efficacy.
