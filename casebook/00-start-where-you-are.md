# Start where you are

You do not need a complete picture before you can improve a situation. You need
an account good enough to take a next step, and a way for reality and other people
to correct it.

Here is a small, **invented example**. It demonstrates a way of reasoning, not
an observed success or a tested intervention.

## Begin with something worth making possible

You help organise a neighbourhood meeting room. Several people have said they
would like somewhere to draw together. The room is available on Thursday
evenings. You could offer a free drawing evening: bring a pencil, share a table,
learn from one another. Nobody has to produce anything impressive.

Your first account is reasonable:

> People want this, the room is free, and I can open it. I'll announce a weekly
> evening and see who comes.

That account connects a wish to some available means. It is enough to begin
investigating. It is not yet enough to promise a dependable weekly gathering.

## Let one fact change the shape

Before announcing it, you speak with the person who normally closes the building.
They can help this Thursday, but afterwards their caring commitments make
evenings difficult. Your plan had quietly relied on their continuing labour.

The room being available and the gathering being supportable are different
facts. You control the announcement; you do not control another person's time.
You could keep the weekly promise by doing everything yourself, but you have
not yet decided whether that is sustainable either.

The new fact does not make the idea foolish. It changes the shape of a workable
beginning.

## Revise the next step

You propose one evening, with the building custodian's agreement on opening and
closing responsibilities. The invitation says that it is a first gathering,
with no ongoing schedule yet. You ask those interested about useful times and
what would make attendance possible, without requiring personal explanations
or volunteering as the price of being welcome.

| First account | Revised account |
|---|---|
| A free room makes a weekly gathering possible. | A room is one part; dependable opening and closing still need an arrangement. |
| Interest means Thursday should work. | Interest is real, but attendance and timing remain uncertain. |
| Announce the series, then solve difficulties. | Offer one clearly bounded evening, learn, and decide whether to continue. |

This is a smaller commitment, but it still creates something: a chance to meet,
draw and discover whether people want to make more of it together. A different
time, another venue or no further event can remain legitimate outcomes.

## Notice what actually happens

Suppose the first evening goes ahead. People arriving would establish that some
people could attend on that occasion. It would not establish that everyone who
wanted to come could do so, that everybody enjoyed it, or that weekly evenings
are sustainable. A quiet room might mean absorbed drawing, discomfort or simply
tiredness; you need more than your preferred interpretation.

A brief conversation afterwards may be enough. What worked? What was difficult?
Does anyone want another evening? The closer can say the arrangement was too
much work. A participant can suggest a change or decline to return. Those who
did not attend need not explain themselves. There is no attendance outcome in
this invented example: the next account belongs to what actually happens.

## Carry the movement, not a form

Start with the possibility you care about. Follow the connection from intention
to something people could actually use. Find an assumption whose failure would
change your next step. Give someone affected a practical way to correct you.
Then act at a scale that fits what you know and can responsibly support.

In Mechanical Ethics, this touches **accounts, carriers, control and exposure,
clocks, correction and maintenance**. You can learn those names later. Here the
important movement was from “I can announce it” to “we can make one evening
work without silently promising someone else's labour.”

Ordinary good judgement may already do this. The proposed value of the language
is to help you hold these relationships together and carry them into less
familiar situations. Whether it helps you do that is something you can challenge.
