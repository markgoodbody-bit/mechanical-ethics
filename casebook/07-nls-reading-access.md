# Case 5. The National Library Service: building and maintaining a usable reading route

*Historical and continuing constructive public-service study; United States, 1931–2026. Prepared 27 October 2026 from named public records. This is an **online addition after the dated 26 October three-case PDF**, not an amendment to the frozen Mechanical Ethics v1.0.0 book, an accessibility evaluation by patrons, or a determination of anyone's legal entitlement.*

## 1. Begin with what becomes possible

An accessible book is not simply a text converted to another format. Somebody must select or produce it, make it discoverable, lend it to an eligible reader, supply a way to play or read it, offer support if the device fails, and keep the arrangement working as formats and people's circumstances change. A public library service can form a durable shared capability: a person can read materials that would otherwise be inaccessible through standard print, without having to purchase a bespoke library of their own.

The U.S. Library of Congress's National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) provides an actual long-running example. Established under federal legislation in 1931, it lends audio and braille materials and the equipment needed to use them without charging eligible patrons. Materials can travel digitally through BARD or physically through a network of cooperating libraries and postage-free mail. The Library describes a national network coordinated across federal, state and local levels. [N1, N2] This is a real service and an important positive achievement; the case does not attribute its creation to Mechanical Ethics.

Our question is narrower: **what joins a formally available right or service to an actually usable and maintained capability, and what changed when independent scrutiny revealed an access barrier?**

## 2. Boundary, affected people and evidence routes

The analysis follows a prospective reader's journey from becoming aware of NLS to receiving readable material. It also follows two changes: the 2016 amendment permitting equipment for electronic braille, and the 2021 expansion of professionals able to certify eligibility. [G1, R1]

The relevant entities have different powers and dependencies:

| Role | Contributes to reading access | Cannot by itself establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Congress and the Library of Congress | Statutory scope, funding and programme direction | That a particular reader knows how to use the service |
| NLS headquarters | Accessible materials, device policy, production and technology | That every applicant can complete local registration |
| State/local network libraries | Applications, enrolment, distribution, reader advice and equipment support | That federal format policy matches every patron's situation |
| USPS and delivery network | Postage-free movement of physical media and devices | That downloaded material or electronic equipment is suitable |
| Eligibility certifiers | A permitted route for establishing qualification | That a certified patron will have useful devices, materials or support |
| Potential patrons and patrons | Needs, reading choices and evidence of practical use | The national eligibility denominator or programme-wide outcomes |
| GAO and other reviewers | Independent challenge and documented recommendations | That a formal recommendation marked implemented caused increased reading |

These are not interchangeable participants. A central agency may have the power to broaden the certification rules while local libraries carry much of the work of applications, circulation, equipment repairs and direct advice. The NLS account says state, local and/or federal sources fund network libraries; one central appropriation therefore should not be passed off as the programme's entire social and operating cost. [N1, N2]

This case does not interview or collect data about individual disabilities, reading preferences or rejected applications. The gap between a rule and lived access remains a **question for affected readers and domain owners**, not a story we invent on their behalf.

## 3. The institutional barrier GAO actually observed

GAO's 4 April 2016 audit examined NLS's services and use of changing technology. It recorded two concrete limits. First, someone seeking service for a reading disability such as dyslexia generally faced a **medical-doctor certification condition** that applicants with other qualifying disabilities did not face. GAO recorded concerns from network library officials and others that the extra steps and costs obstructed access. GAO interviewed eight selected network libraries; their experience is material evidence but not a nationally representative measurement of all eligible people. [G1]

Second, the statute then constrained using NLS funds to provide equipment for electronic braille, even though NLS already made electronic braille material available. A file could exist while the funded route to a necessary reading device remained outside the programme's authority. The solution could not consist solely of teaching librarians to be more helpful: one missing capability was a legislative permission to purchase and lend a different class of equipment. [G1]

Notice how these two barriers operate at different points. The certification rule concerns **entry**; the equipment rule concerns **use after entry**. A single headline such as 'accessible library exists' loses both distinctions. A bare count of registered people also cannot reveal everyone deterred before registration.

## 4. Two corrections and two clocks

| Event | What is established by the named source | What does **not** follow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| April 2016: GAO report | Independent federal audit reports certification and device-authorisation issues; recommendations and a matter for Congress [G1]. | Every eligible reader was counted or personally interviewed. |
| July 2016: Public Law 114-219 | Congress amended the equipment authorization; GAO's status page describes the matter as implemented [G2]. | Actual distribution or individual use followed immediately. |
| February 2021: certification rule | The final rule expanded permitted certifiers to include educators, psychologists, certain nurses and other qualifying professionals, and removed the special medical-doctor requirement for reading disabilities [R1]. | Previously excluded applicants all enrolled or their experience was measured. |
| 2024: braille-device rollout | NLS reports nationwide distribution to network libraries with staff training and approximately **8,500** eReaders with patrons and network libraries [N3]. | Those are 8,500 unique newly served people or an effect size caused by GAO. |
| FY2025 reported in 2026 | NLS says nearly **17,000** of its new DA2 talking-book players had been distributed and a separate VA sign-up collaboration added nearly **3,000** patrons [N4]. | These figures quantify the effect of the 2021 certification change or service for all eligible people. |

The equipment authority changed within months of the audit. The certification rule changed years later. The evidence does not support a single 'the programme was fixed' timestamp. There are at least four distinct clocks: legislation, rulemaking, physical capability distribution and individual practical access. The first two can be observed in published acts and rules. The last needs patron-level evidence that the published records here do not supply.

GAO's status page marks its five listed congressional/executive recommendations as **closed/implemented** and describes follow-up studies, an eligibility pilot and other administrative actions. [G2] That is meaningful evidence of specified follow-up activity and legal change. It is not an independent field measurement of how many reading opportunities were added or whose access failed afterwards.

## 5. The complete usable route is composed, not declared

For a particular reader and item at a particular time, a successful route might require:

1. **Discover:** the reader learns the service exists through a route they can actually use.
2. **Qualify:** the person meets the applicable eligibility conditions.
3. **Certify:** someone authorized and accessible can complete the relevant certification without excessive delay or cost.
4. **Enrol:** the network library processes the application and tells the patron the next step.
5. **Obtain:** the desired item exists in an appropriate medium and can be delivered or downloaded.
6. **Use:** the patron has appropriate equipment, training or assistance to read the item.
7. **Continue:** equipment, repairs, software, staff support and continued lending survive over time.

These are analytical dependencies, not a checklist NLS claims to have adopted. They expose multiple useful intervention points. Improving certification can remove an entry barrier, while a missing device can still block practical use. Providing a device can widen possibilities, while an online-only route could impose a new burden on someone without reliable internet. The programme's combination of physical lending and downloads is therefore relevant: new technological options need not erase older useful options. [N1, N2, N3]

Formally, for one declared reading goal, we can denote the composed route as a conjunction of necessary usable transitions. Missing evidence for one necessary step yields **unestablished completion**, not a verdict that all service has failed. If a helpful neighbour, network librarian or alternate format establishes a different route, the model must represent it rather than infer none exist.

Neither a federal rule nor a device shipment certifies end-user usability. The local network and the user's own account are necessary additional apertures. Equally, a measured service benefit is not disproved by a missing evaluation of one subgroup.

## 6. Who carries the continuing work?

NLS is a *constructive* case, rather than just a failure-and-appeal story. Its persistent capability depends on continuing contributions: choosing and producing accessible books, making delivery systems work, repairing machines, answering questions, certifying applicants and renewing formats. The service is valuable because people perform those functions repeatedly, not because an act was passed once in 1931. [N1, N2]

The burden ledger is consequently plural.

- NLS carries central content, policy and device-development work.
- Local network libraries carry registration, circulation, equipment loans and reader-support work, funded from a combination of government sources.
- USPS carries a separate physical distribution function.
- Potential and actual patrons can still carry effort in finding the service, navigating eligibility, learning technology or reporting failure.

The fourth item is an analytical possibility, **not** a quantified burden study of patrons. The first three are described by the operators' public records. Without an audited total of central, network, postal and unpaid work, it would be misleading to report a single 'cost per reading opportunity' from NLS's central budget alone.

Maintenance may also create new choices. A local library that lends hardware with support can help a patron use a new format without requiring that patron to master every implementation detail or transfer control of their reading history to a platform. Whether a particular patron prefers downloaded, posted, digital audio or braille material remains that patron's question.

## 7. Reading the positive results without inventing causation

The strongest **observed** positive result in this bounded source set is not a modelled saving. NLS reports an operating national lending service and actual nationwide braille eReader distribution with staff training. GAO's identified legal barrier to lending that equipment was subsequently changed. [G1, G2, N1, N3]

That sequence supports a *possibility-enabling institutional mechanism*: change the authorisation, deliver equipment and maintain the network that can make it usable. It does **not** establish that the change caused an independently estimated increase in literacy, enjoyment, autonomy, patron health or enrolment. None of those outcomes was measured by this case.

The 2025 reported VA sign-up and player distribution demonstrate additional activity and access routes [N4], but those are operator reports about distinct initiatives. They are not independent replications, and they do not directly quantify the effect of the 2021 rule. Likewise, fewer patrons in one year could reflect many competing explanations; we have not sourced a consistent population denominator or eligibility breakdown for a causal trend analysis.

One protected negative is important: GAO expressly warned that the **precise number of eligible people was unknown**. [G1] Without a defensible denominator, statements about nationwide coverage rates cannot be made from reported patron totals alone. Publicly available applications and circulation counts can still help improve a service without proving every potential reader was reached.

## 8. Strongest ordinary method, proposed comparative use

This case already has a competent and stronger domain method: GAO's programme evaluation, legislative change processes, accessible-service standards and network-library practice. These processes found the concrete barriers without Mechanical Ethics. We therefore **must not** credit the framework with either discovery or repair.

A meaningful future comparison could give an accessibility professional and a consenting eligible reader the ordinary NLS evaluation route first. Record how they already assess application certification, multiple formats, device availability, service maintenance and the outcome meaningful to that reader. Then use ME's five questions to see whether it adds an overlooked *reachable* correction route, an unacknowledged network cost, or a useful new choice.

This comparison can legitimately return **EXISTING_METHOD_BETTER**, **NO_MATERIAL_DIFFERENCE**, **USEFUL_ADDITION** or **INDETERMINATE**. It would require participant consent, independent assessment, careful handling of personal information and no implication that sharing testimony is required to receive assistance. The present chapter reports **no participant** and no such finding.

## 9. What this case contributes — and what stays open

The NLS example broadens the online casebook beyond institutions failing to protect people in time. It shows a public capability built and maintained for decades, independent audit identifying specific barriers, genuine legislative and regulatory corrections, and operator-reported distribution of new means of access.

A practical distinction survives: **opening a legal door, delivering a tool, and a person actually being able to use it are different transitions**. These transitions can be joined constructively, with costs and authority distributed across organisations. But the records used here cannot tell us how a particular person's life changed after the reforms, or whether ME is better than existing accessibility practice at seeing the relevant steps.

An independent, affected reader's experience and strong specialist method remain the next owners of those questions. No moral standing, entitlement, eligibility outcome or advice about a present application is decided by this account.
