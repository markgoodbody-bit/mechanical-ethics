# Mechanical Ethics v0.8 beta — intellectual neighbours

Status: **BETA SUPPORT / ATTRIBUTION MAP / NOT A CLAIM OF OWNERSHIP, NOVELTY, OR CONFORMANCE**.

Mechanical Ethics is intentionally synthetic. Where an older or stronger body of work already carries a structure well, the correct move is to credit it, learn from it, and decide whether ME should import a compact distinction, interoperate with the owner, or simply point outward.

## Hope / agency / viable routes

- C. R. Snyder et al., “The Will and the Ways: Development and Validation of an Individual-Differences Measure of Hope” (1991). Snyder's hope theory separates agency and pathways around goals; this is a major neighbour for the human language of routes and action.
- Victoria McGeer, “The Art of Good Hope” (2004). McGeer treats hope as closely tied to agency and explores how hope can be scaffolded socially rather than treated only as an isolated internal feeling.
- Jean-Pierre Aubin's viability theory. Viability kernels are the mathematical neighbour of ME Appendix A's bounded viability set.

ME does not claim these works are equivalent. Its narrower refusal is that a supported route does not prove a feeling of hope, and absence of a hopeful feeling does not prove that no route exists.

## Capability / real opportunity

- Amartya Sen's capability approach, especially the distinction between resources and substantive opportunities a person can actually convert into doings and beings. This strongly neighbours ME's insistence that a door on paper is not enough if it cannot be found, afforded, used or integrated.

ME does not adopt a universal list of valued capabilities or infer that more options are always morally better.

## Privacy / contextual information flows

- Helen Nissenbaum, “Privacy as Contextual Integrity” (2004), and *Privacy in Context* (2009/2010). Contextual integrity is a strong neighbour for the claim that information access and disclosure are governed by purpose, role and context rather than by a single public/private switch.

`PRIVATE != DECEPTIVE` is therefore a reader-facing disambiguation, not a claim that ME invented privacy theory.

## Formation / habituation

- John Dewey, *Human Nature and Conduct* (1922), especially habit as interaction between organism and environment and the way conduct helps form later dispositions.
- Older virtue and habituation traditions, including Aristotle, are obvious neighbours to the idea that repeated conduct can alter later action.

ME keeps the claim deliberately thinner: supported change in capability, relationship, constraint or selection conditions does not establish a total character, essence or destiny.

## Exit, voice and cooperation

- Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970), for the distinction between leaving an arrangement and changing it from within.
- Elinor Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990), for long-lived cooperative arrangements with boundaries, participant rule-making, monitoring and conflict-resolution structures.

These are strong neighbours for refusal, answer-back, maintenance and joint construction. Cooperation still does not establish consent to every consequence.

## Machine future-space / preserving options

- Victoria Krakovna et al., “Penalizing side effects using stepwise relative reachability” (2018), which makes baseline choice load-bearing and documents bad incentives from naive preservation of reversibility.
- Alexander Matt Turner, Dylan Hadfield-Menell & Prasad Tadepalli, “Conservative Agency via Attainable Utility Preservation” (2019/2020), which examines preserving future attainable utility under uncertain objectives.

These systems are useful pressure on ME's future-space language precisely because they show that preserving more reachability is not automatically equivalent to a better future.
